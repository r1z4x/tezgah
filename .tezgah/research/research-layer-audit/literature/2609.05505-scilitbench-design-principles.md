## Claim, with its exact numbers

Screening reliability is decided by the *protocol text*, and the effects are
measurable:

- making the inclusion criteria explicit improves average F2 by **17.0%** over a
  generic classification prompt; adding **exclusion reasoning** brings the total
  gain to **28.8%**;
- adding "researcher rationales" - a decision paired with **quoted evidence** and
  a concise justification - to few-shot examples raises average F2 from **0.551 to
  0.633** (a 15% relative improvement); the paper's stated design principle is
  that "when inclusion criteria are nuanced, examples are most useful when they
  include the reasoning that produced the decision, not just the decision itself";
- the best single configuration reaches F2 **0.981** at perfect recall
  (precision 0.910), and no ensembling configuration beat the single-model
  baseline.

## Why it matters here

The workspace records literature as free-form notes with no inclusion criteria and
no per-source decision. This paper says the cheapest single improvement to
screening quality is writing the criteria and the exclusion reasons down, with the
quoted evidence for each decision - and each of those three is a *field*, i.e.
something a checker can require. E4 measured the current state: 0 of 19 synthesis
bullets name a source in a resolvable form, and no artifact compares sources.
