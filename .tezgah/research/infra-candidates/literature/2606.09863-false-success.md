# From Confident Closing to Silent Failure: Characterizing False Success in LLM Agents

- id: alphaXiv 2606.09863 (arXiv preprint), 2026-06-01
- author: Laksh Advani (University of Colorado)
- url: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.09863
- read: alphaXiv report, 2026-09-19 (report, not the PDF)

## Claim

**False success**: the agent's closing message asserts completion while the
programmatic environment state says the task failed. It is worse than a crash
because the interaction *looks* resolved, so the failure propagates silently.

Two corpora, and the second one is the important one for us:

- **tau2-bench**: 9,876 trajectories from 8 frontier model families; 1,730
  failure trajectories; labels derived from the final message by regex, with 200
  hand-validated (91.5% agreement, Cohen's kappa 0.86).
- **AppWorld**: 8,190 trajectories; the agent writes an explicit `status` field
  to a supervisor database, *independent of its natural language*, and the
  programmatic unit tests grade the environment. A trajectory is false success
  when `status=success` meets a failing evaluation. 1,879 trajectories
  (1,425 false success, 454 honest failure). **The label shares no text features
  with the agent's reply.**

## The finding that matters

**LLM judges systematically fail to detect false success.** The paper frames
this as the reason a text-level judge is the wrong instrument, and shows a
lightweight detector (TF-IDF + logistic regression over bigrams) doing the job
instead. They control for circularity by masking trigger tokens and training on
non-closing text.

## Why it matters here

tezgah's Stop rule is already on the right side of this result: it never reads
the reply to decide *whether* work happened, it reads the ledger
(`docs/evidence.md`, "the trigger for 2-4 is the turn's own evidence, not its
words"). The paper supplies the external justification for that design and, more
usefully, the **measurement definition** tezgah's own `false_completion` counter
is a weaker version of:

- tezgah counts a refusal to stop (`blocked`) as false completion, where the
  refusal can fire for "no check ran at all".
- Advani's definition requires an *environment state* that contradicts the
  claim - the arm-bench `hidden/` checks are exactly that independent grader, and
  `bench.py report` already computes CPS and pass rate from them.

So the sharpest version of tezgah's headline number is not
`false_completion / claims` over its own ledger, it is: **of the runs whose
hidden checks failed, what share ended with a reply claiming success.** That
number is computable today from `benchmarks/arm-bench/results/*.jsonl` plus the
transcripts, and no session has computed it.
