# 2608.03571 — Beyond Simply Environment Scaling: Designing Effective Environment Distributions for Multimodal Agent Learning

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03571 — Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Dongqi Huang, Hongbang Yuan,
Yupu Hao, Kang Liu, Jun Zhao (Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; UCAS) —
2026-08-04, cs.CV. Report read in full; the arXiv abstract was fetched as the second record; code
at https://github.com/GaryStack/Beyond-MMEnv-Scaling.

## What it is
The counter-argument in its most explicit form, and the only source in this set whose title is
the claim: "simply increasing the number of multimodal environments does not always benefit"
(abstract). The authors train on incrementally larger subsets of a 200-environment pool at a
fixed compute budget and find the success rate "does not monotonically improve with an increasing
number of environment types, sometimes even degrading". They then build two replacements:
Ability-aware Environment Selection (AES), which selects environments by the *abilities* their
rollouts exercise, using atomic-ability segmentation (via GPT-5), redundancy control and
gradient-conflict control; and a Hierarchical Difficulty Curriculum (HDC) over harness weakening
and state-scale progression.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- Selection beats size: training on the 30 AES-selected environments "consistently outperformed
  training on the full pool of 170 environments (All Envs) and a randomly selected subset of 30
  environments (Random-K). For the 4B model, AES achieved an average relative gain of 95.6% over
  the base model, compared to 43.4% for All Envs and 44.6% for Random-K."
- Negative transfer is measurable and worse for multimodal inputs: mixed training on the
  multimodal version "resulted in a significantly larger performance drop (10.7%) compared to the
  text-symbolic version (1.3%)".
- Ablations isolate *why* selection works: removing redundancy control "reduced the relative gain
  on OOD environments from 40.3% to 25.3%"; removing conflict control "reduced it even further to
  2.8%".
- Surface-level diversity measures fail: selection by textual descriptions scores "2.8% relative
  gain" and by code embeddings "19.5%", against AES's ability-based selection.
- Combined AES + HDC: "the highest average relative gain of 143.2% over the base model".

## Why it is in this line
It is the required counter-argument, and it is the strongest one in this set because it is
measured rather than argued: a 30-environment selected pool beats a 170-environment full pool,
and the mechanism is optimisation conflict (polarised gradient correlations), not taste. It is
also the one that maps onto a small corpus most directly - if a pool of 170 already contains
environments that hurt, a repository with a handful of tasks should be selecting rather than
adding. Its "harness weakening" axis is the same move the environment-side adaptation in this
line proposes, under the paper's own name for it.

## Quality and limits
Formal: arXiv preprint, CAS Institute of Automation and UCAS, corresponding author named, code
repository named, no venue or peer review visible in the record read. Scope is multimodal
(visual) agents and Qwen3-VL-4B/8B, so the 10.7% negative-transfer figure is a multimodal
figure; the report does not claim it transfers to text-only agents. Every number is the authors'
own, cited and not reproduced here.
