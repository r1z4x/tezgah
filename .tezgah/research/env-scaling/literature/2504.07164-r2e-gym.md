# 2504.07164 — R2E-Gym: Procedural Environments and Hybrid Verifiers for Scaling Open-Weights SWE Agents

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164 — Naman Jain, Jaskirat Singh, Manish Shetty, Liang Zheng,
Koushik Sen, Ion Stoica — 2025-04-09, cs.SE / cs.CL / cs.LG. Report read in full; the arXiv
abstract was fetched as the second record; code at https://github.com/R2E-Gym/R2E-Gym.

## What it is
The second named generation pipeline, and the one that pairs procedural environment curation
with a verifier study. SYNGEN mines commits, extracts the code change and build scripts,
generates or collects Fail-to-Pass tests, and back-translates a problem statement from change
plus tests - so neither a human-written issue nor a human-written unit test is required. The
verifier half is a comparison of two test-time scaling axes: execution-based verifiers and
execution-free verifiers, and a hybrid of the two.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- "AgentGym, the largest procedurally-curated executable gym environment for training real-world
  SWE-agents, consisting of more than 8.7K tasks" (abstract; the body calls the same artifact
  R2E-Gym).
- "a PASS@1 of 34.4% on SWE-BENCH-VERIFIED" for the 32B model trained on it.
- Verifier saturation: "while each approach individually saturates around 42-43%, significantly
  higher gains can be obtained by leveraging their complementary strengths"; the hybrid reaches
  "51% on the SWE-Bench Verified benchmark".
- The failure modes named for each verifier are the ones a reward model inherits: test-based
  verifiers "suffer from low distinguishability", execution-free verifiers "are biased and often
  rely on stylistic features".

## Why it is in this line
Two reasons. First, it is one of the three named generation pipelines, and it is the one whose
companion artifact is a *verifier* rather than a corpus - the prerequisite the repository-side
answer has to confront, because execution-based verification is what makes a rollout scoreable.
Second, its hybrid-verifier result is the earliest source in this set for the claim the
counter-argument sources push further: adding environments (or rollouts) saturates, while
changing what scores them does not.
