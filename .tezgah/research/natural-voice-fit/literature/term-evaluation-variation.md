# Improving term evaluation in machine translation - variation matters (arXiv 2609.08779)

**What it is and claims.** Terminology evaluation usually assumes one correct target form per source term, but human translators vary, and current metrics penalise that variation as inconsistency. The paper combines glossary-based accuracy, translation consistency and a cross-term variation diagnostic, over English-French scientific corpora from four MT systems, and finds that MT systems produce less target-side variation than human translators do, with transfer depending on the variation type.

**How it changes the skill.** It supplies the measurable check for both new axes: glossary accuracy and consistency for the no-translate rule, and cross-term variation as a way to see the flattening that our marker-density number is a crude version of. Less variation than a human is a measurement, not a taste.
