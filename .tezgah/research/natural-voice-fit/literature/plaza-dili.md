# Plaza dili (the term the user called "plazma dili")

**What it is and claims.** Two independent searches in session agreed on the shape of the answer. **"plazma dili" is not a
linguistic or dictionary term** - Turkish `plazma` has only the physics and blood senses - **but it is
attested in colloquial use**, including a school magazine hosted on the Turkish Ministry of National
Education domain that reports a panel on language corruption covering `plazma dili`. The standard
name is **"plaza dili" / "plaza Türkçesi"**: the Turkish of office towers, where
the English term is kept because it sounds better than the Turkish one and Turkish is deliberately
swollen around it (byakalin.com, plazacubes.com, pazarlamaturkiye.com, plannet.com.tr, eksisozluk,
preply.com). All six are popular or commercial writing, not scholarship. The scholarly neighbours
are the code-switching and loanword literature - an integrated borrowing belongs to the host
language and only a clause-level switch is mixed speech (literature/loanword-or-switch.md) - and
the terminology literature, which treats a term's target form as a per-term decision with a
glossary rail rather than a style preference (literature/term-evaluation-variation.md).

**How it changes the skill.** It separates three cases the current skill cannot tell apart, all on
one axis - keep or translate, decided per term and per register:
1. **Deliberate retention (plaza dili).** Business English kept inside Turkish corporate prose.
   This is a register feature, not a defect: a skill editing for a Turkish corporate reader who
   bans it produces text that reads wrong to that reader.
2. **Terms that must stay verbatim.** Standardised vocabulary (ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765 in software, the
   sources for legal and medical terms), identifiers, commands, paths, product and brand names,
   units and standards designations - the do-not-translate class (llm-unreliable-se-terminology).
3. **The actual defect - calques.** Not a kept term but a term *translated literally*: `aksiyon
   almak` for take action, `fokus olmak` for focus. This is what reads as machine text, and it is
   sourced per language (alpay-motamo-ceviri).

**Second record.** Turkish Wikipedia's "Plaza dili" entry defines it as a Turkish register carrying largely foreign, especially English, loans and calques, associated with white-collar workers, and records a 2018 survey of 120 such workers in which 80 (67%) agreed a shared plaza language exists, the criticism of the Turkish Language Association president (2019) and an industrialist who called it `uydurukça` and fined its use in meeting rooms. That is the phenomenon named by its own speakers, still not scholarship.

**Quality.** The term itself is attested only in grey and popular sources, so the note records the
phenomenon as a named register in practice and puts the policy on the scholarly sources above; no
scholarly source in this session used "plaza dili" as a technical term.
