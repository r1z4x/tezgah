# 2609.20519 — SoL-Pi: Recursively Scaling Auto-Research Loops for Efficient Agent Harness

NVIDIA / NTU / MIT, 2026-09-17. arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20519, read through orx
(the arXiv abstract record, the arXiv HTML body, and the alphaXiv report) on 2026-09-22;
the same record is at https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.20519. The companion repository's
shipped code is a separate note (`sol-pi-nvlabs-harness-extensions.md`) because the two
sources carry different objects: this one carries the loop, that one carries the four

## What it is

A protocol for scaling auto-research loops over agent harnesses, and the evidence from
running it. Four parts matter to this line.

**The acceptance rule (§2.1).** Before experimentation begins, "capability metrics,
acceptable tolerances, and efficiency metrics are fixed and remain unchanged throughout
the search", and they are "strictly isolated from the optimizing agent's control to prevent
it from gaming the acceptance criteria". A candidate then has to pass two sequential gates:
"every capability metric must remain within its predeclared tolerance, and the candidate
must improve at least one declared efficiency metric". Among the candidates that pass both,
"the pipeline retains the nondominated results under the declared metrics".

**The pre-rollout screen and the cycle (§2.2).** Of 152 proposed directions in six families
(context, progress, tools, delegation, prompt & policy, improvement & evaluation), a
pre-screening stage - the paper's Oracle Analysis, which "examines existing development
trajectories to identify avoidable work in the base harness" - filters them "before the
system spends rollout budget". Each surviving direction runs the Karpathy autoresearch
cycle - "propose a change, implement it, run a fixed experiment, inspect the result, and
retain, revise, or discard" - with a Ralph-Loop implementer, "an independent reviewer", a
map-reduce trajectory-analysis stage, and "a disposable instance of a shared skill
template" whose modified orchestration code is discarded per experiment. The blog
quantifies the screen's output: adjacent edit/write-to-command candidates at 12.3% of
cross-turn transitions, Bash at 85.1% of observed next actions, and a projected 11.5% token
reduction under full triggering.

**The scale and the hold-out (§1, §2.3, §2.5).** ~535 executable search environments - 495
derived from GitHub issue-PR pairs, kept only when "the test fails before the patch and
passes afterward", and 40 verifier-driven synthetic ones - against more than 3,000 runs and
more than 60,000 agent-environment interactions, in total 1.10 B tokens. Evaluation is on
EdgeBench, held out: "Held-out results never feed back into the Auto-Research Loops: a
failed validation rejects the candidate without triggering further optimization", with 11
of 51 public tasks used for one-way acceptance of frozen candidates and the remaining 40
reserved for final evaluation.

**The result, as the authors state it (§3).** 44.7-49.0% less recorded token traffic than
Pi at 93.7-94.3% of Pi's score, with "API cost by about one third"; Terminal-Bench 4: 15/63
solved at $211.12 against Pi's 18/63 at $286.45; and the paper concedes the cost in §5.1 -
"Running complete auto-research loops in our environment is computationally expensive" -
leaving the scaling laws to future work.

## Why it is in this line

It is the formal source for the three ideas this line borrows, and it is also the sharpest
statement of the difference between a research *result* and a research *loop*: the
protocol, the gates and the hold-out rule are here in prose, while the repository ships
only the four mechanisms that survived. The line's question - what does either repository
carry that tezgah could use - therefore reads this paper for its rules and the repository
for its code, and finds the rules better than the code.

## Quality and limits

**Formal**: an arXiv preprint with a stated author and affiliation list, fetched through
orx. No venue or peer review is visible in the record read. Every figure above is the
authors' own, measured on their own hardware and at their own prices ("API prices as of
August 17, 2026"); nothing was reproduced here - this line installed nothing, ran nothing
and spent nothing - so the numbers are cited, never measured. EdgeBench is an external
project that was not obtained, so the held-out half of the protocol was read and not
checked. The loop's artifacts - the Oracle Analysis prompt, the acceptance-gate code, the
environment builder, the lineage scheduler - are not published anywhere in the repository,
so each rule above is a statement about a method this survey could read and not about code
it could inspect.
