# English Style Guide, European Commission DG Translation (8th ed., 2025)

**What it is and claims.** Three keep rules and the counter-case: reproduce a quotation or a document title rather than re-styling it; an untranslated foreign abbreviation keeps its original capitalisation; scientific symbols are interlingual and need no adaptation; unit symbols take no plural. Foreign words are italicised with their accents while assimilated ones stay roman, Latin abbreviations always roman, and Latin phrases are never hyphenated - and the guide then says that `per diem` and many others have English equivalents that should be preferred.

**How it changes the skill.** The same authority that keeps some strings tells you when the keep rule loses: an assimilated loan is not a foreign word, and a loan with a plain equivalent should be replaced. That is the counter-case the skill needs so the keep list cannot become a blanket ban on translating.

**Quality.** Institutional or vendor source read in session; prescriptive, not peer-reviewed.
