## Claim

Evidence about an agent's process has to be *structured* before it can be
assessed, and the paper states the failure directly: **"a hash does not establish
semantic truth, a signature does not establish authorization, and an external
stamp does not establish that the data are relevant."** Its model separates the
roles that produce evidence from the roles that assess it - the audit function
"should not be controlled only by the same agents and running" the work - and
gives each record an explicit evidence-claim structure (production, canonical
record, classification, audit assessment, management response) rather than
leaving its evidentiary meaning implicit.

## Why it matters here

Two consequences for the layer, both mechanical:

1. The producer and the assessor are the same session. `check` is run by the
   session that wrote the line, and the six-dimension review is prose the session
   writes about itself; the one independent artifact - the commit graph - is the
   one E2 shows is unavailable in the prescribed layout.
2. "A path exists" carries exactly the weight a hash does in this paper: it is an
   integrity statement, not a relevance statement. E1's P4-P6 are the measurement
   of that gap in this codebase.
