cs.CL / cs.AI. Report read in full; the arXiv abstract was fetched as the second record; code at

## What it is
The strongest form of the generation pipeline: the environment itself is a learnable component
of post-training. One LLM plays two roles from the same weights - an Environment Designer that
writes a complete environment as executable Python with a Gym-style `reset()`/`step()` interface,
and a Reasoning Agent that solves it. The Environment Designer's reward is the agent's *regret*:
the gap between its reward with a privileged hint and without it, blended with a difficulty
anchor that prefers environments where the agent's win rate is in a target band; environments
are validated for executability before entering the pool; a corpus-grounding step and an
accumulated environment memory keep the supply novel.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- At 30B scale, "SPADE improves over the strongest fixed-environment baseline by +5.3 on average
  across eight held-out math, science, code, and reasoning benchmarks, and lifts the tool-use
  setting by +5.7 on BFCL-v4 multi-turn and +13.9 on ACEBench-Agent" (abstract). The report adds
  "+8.1 points over the untrained base model" for the same 30B-A3B model and states the margin
  over the fixed-environment baseline "grows with model scale".
- Environment supply: the "learnable share" - win rate between 20% and 80% - "increased over
  training, reaching approximately one-third of the generated environments by the end of a
  400-step run".
- Diversity depends on grounding, not on volume: without corpus grounding the run "resulted in
  significant mode collapse (Vendi/n score of 0.04 compared to 0.68 with corpus grounding)".
- Curriculum breadth: "A broader 6-skill curriculum significantly outperformed a restricted
  2-skill curriculum, capturing approximately twice the gains on Reasoning-Gym benchmarks".
- Ablations: removing Environment Designer training and memory "resulted in performance worse
  than the untrained base model"; training on a static pool from a stronger external model
  "recovered only a fraction of SPADE's overall improvement".

## Why it is in this line
It is the source that shows what environment scaling costs when it is done properly: the
environment becomes a *trained* artifact, its reward is the agent's regret, and its diversity is
maintained by grounding rather than by increasing the count. For a repository with no training
loop this is the clearest negative boundary - the strongest version of the axis requires two
trained policies and a rollout budget. Its mode-collapse and curriculum-breadth findings are
also the quantitative form of the counter-argument: more environments without grounding is
measurably less diverse.
