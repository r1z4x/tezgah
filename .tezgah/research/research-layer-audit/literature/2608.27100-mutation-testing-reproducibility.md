## Claim, and the method this audit borrowed

The paper's premise is the one this line's E1 tests: **"existing repository
validation workflows may execute successfully without detecting changes to such
choices"** - random seed, dependency pin, data split, cross-validation fold
count.

Method: 39 frozen repository-operator cases, each mutated in exactly one
reproducibility-relevant way, then run against the validation workflow the
repository already has; a mutation counts as detected only when the *unmodified*
baseline executes first, so a broken environment cannot be scored as a catch.

Numbers: primary execution evaluated 13 of 39 cases (33.3%); with a second layer,
24 of 39 (61.5%). Of the mutations that could be evaluated, the
mutation-detection proportion was **2/23 (8.7%)**; by class, random-seed 0/5,
dependency-pin 2/3, data-split 0/4, cv-fold-count 0/3. By validation-workflow
kind: assertion 0/2, completion-only 2/13 (15.4%), documented-example 1/11 (9.1%),
CI 0/1, upstream-test 0/6.

## Why it matters here

This is the external instrument for E1. The audit's eight probes are the same
design applied to a rule checker: each probe mutates exactly one documented rule,
the control establishes that the harness can detect a violation at all (2/2), and
the measured detection proportion is **0/8**. The paper also validates the
reporting shape this line uses - frozen case set, evaluated versus non-evaluable
cases named separately, per-class breakdown rather than one headline rate.
