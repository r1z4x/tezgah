## What it is
The counter-argument at the level of *what the scaling constructs*, not how much of it. Its
claim is that agentic RL "scales training by expanding programmatic environments and synthesizing
diverse tasks" and that "this scaling can paradoxically narrow training coverage through how data
are constructed": pipelines "construct tasks from executable paths or successful workflows",
which induces a "construction-induced selection bias toward feasible requests", so requests that
are blocked, conditionally feasible, or feasible only after a compliant revision are
systematically absent. Its fix is to make the unit of construction the *scenario* -
environment instance, initial request, simulated user, reward criteria - with conflict scenarios
co-designed from the environment's own constraints and a structured oracle recording what the
agent must do, explain, revise or refuse.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- Environment construction: "3,039 initial environment candidates and 2,415 retained
  environments" after tool-level execution repair and stateful execution testing, where
  "Environments are retained when at least 85% of calls receive pass or warning labels".
- Dataset: "4,513 Multi-Step scenarios and 4,612 Multi-Turn scenarios. The Multi-Turn set includes
  2,360 feasible and 2,252 conflict scenarios."
- Results on the 8B model against its Qwen3-8B base: "τ²-Bench average increases from 34.3% to
  59.1%, a gain of 24.8 points. ACEBench-Agent average increases from 54.6% to 81.0%, a gain of
  26.4 points. BFCL-V4 average increases from 50.3% to 57.0%, a gain of 6.7 points."
- On the 30B model against its base: τ²-Bench "52.3% to 66.4%", ACEBench-Agent "72.9% to 78.3%",
  BFCL-V4 "56.7% to 61.0%".
- The behaviour the conflict data buys: Multi-Turn training "raises Block performance
  substantially, such as from 43.2% after Multi-Step training to 71.6% on Airline tasks".

## Why it is in this line
It is the counter-argument that a repository with a fixed, hand-written corpus should take most
seriously, because its object is the *distribution* rather than the count: a corpus built only
from paths known to succeed under-samples the interactions where an agent must notice a conflict
and refuse or renegotiate. Every scenario in this repository's arm-bench was written by someone
who knew it was solvable, which is the exact construction bias named here - and one of this
repository's two published nulls is a task in which all twelve arms passed by amending a
specification to satisfy a conflicting test (the `g02` gate task), an interaction of the kind
this paper says such corpora omit.

## Quality and limits
Grey by record, formal by shape: a full-text report read through alphaXiv, authors and
affiliations stated, but no arXiv id, no OpenAlex record and no venue - so nothing here inherits
peer review, and the numbers are the authors' own throughout. Every figure quoted above is

interpreted cautiously" applies to its 30B-versus-235B remark, which is not quoted here.
