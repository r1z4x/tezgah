### Research Report: Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures

#### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The author of this research paper is Sunny Dubey, identified by ORCID: 0009-0002-8296-8631. No specific institutional affiliation is explicitly stated within the provided document.

#### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development and deployment of Large Language Model (LLM) agents are increasingly common, but these agents are prone to various failures, such as infinite loops, cascading tool errors, goal drift, fabrication of information, or silent absorption of corrupted content. The prevailing method for addressing these failures involves using a second LLM to audit every step, a process that is often more expensive than the agent's operation itself. This work positions itself within the domain of online agent monitoring and failure detection, aiming to provide a more efficient and cost-effective alternative to LLM-as-judge paradigms.

The paper differentiates its approach from several related works:
*   **Foundational Techniques:** It builds upon classical sequential change detection methods like CUSUM, one-class classification, and reservoir computing (specifically Echo-State Networks, ESNs) for its temporal monitoring backbone. Baselines include Mahalanobis distance, isolation forests, and various sequence models (GRU, LSTM, TCN).
*   **Online Agent Failure Detection:** While acknowledging existing efforts in online detection, such as AgentForesight (which uses a 7B LLM auditor per prefix), weakly supervised early alerting, or rule-based runtime guards (AgentSpec, ProbGuard), this work distinguishes itself by its strict adherence to a one-class, label-free, causal-per-step monitoring protocol. It explicitly avoids model internals, external labels for failures, or additional LLM calls for detection, in contrast to methods like PrefixGuard which train prefix-risk scorers supervised on terminal outcomes, or Trajectory Guard which uses Siamese recurrent autoencoders over pre-trained embeddings. The core contribution is framed as the specific combination of these constraints at a microsecond-scale computational cost.
*   **Repair Mechanisms:** The paper also connects to research on repairing failed trajectories, notably AgentTether. While AgentTether diagnoses after a run has failed and re-executes with guidance, this work focuses on mid-episode alarms followed by rollback and re-execution from a checkpoint, with guidance based on the failing check type rather than localized cause.
*   **Limitations of Existing Paradigms:** It explicitly states its independence from LLM-as-judge models (due to cost and bias), hallucination detection methods that require model sampling or internal access, and internal-state monitoring (acknowledging its potential but highlighting the practical scenario where such access is unavailable). Instead, for specific failure types like fabrication, it advocates for deterministic verification methods rather than statistical monitors.

In summary, this research seeks to fill a gap in the landscape by offering a highly efficient, real-time, and resource-lean solution for detecting and repairing LLM agent failures, using only readily observable telemetry and training solely on healthy agent runs.

#### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a lightweight, always-on monitoring system capable of detecting Large Language Model (LLM) agent failures in real-time, specifically mid-episode, without incurring significant computational overhead.

The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges associated with deploying LLM agents:
*   **Prevalence of Failures:** LLM agents frequently encounter various failure modes during execution, including looping, cascading tool errors, deviation from goals (goal drift), fabrication of information, and silent absorption of corrupted content. These failures can lead to incorrect outcomes, wasted computational resources, and a degraded user experience.
*   **Cost of Existing Solutions:** The standard approach to ensure agent reliability involves using a second LLM to act as a "judge" or auditor for every step of the agent's execution. This method is computationally intensive and costly, often exceeding the operational cost of the agent itself. The research aims to explore whether a substantial portion of these failures can be detected through much cheaper means.
*   **Resource Constraints:** Many deployment scenarios may not allow for access to LLM model internals (e.g., hidden states, log probabilities), detailed failure labels for supervised training, or the computational budget for continuous auditing by another LLM. The research is motivated by the need for a solution that operates under these practical constraints.
*   **Timeliness of Intervention:** Detecting failures *mid-episode*, before the task fully fails or the budget is exhausted, allows for timely intervention, such as rollback and re-execution. This can improve task success rates and resource efficiency.
*   **Necessity for Causal Monitoring:** The goal is to develop a causal per-step monitor that can issue alarms based on the trajectory up to the current step, rather than post-hoc analysis or scoring a completed plan.
*   **Empirical Validation:** A significant motivation is to validate the proposed monitoring system at scale within real-world agent ecosystems, using diverse agent frameworks, models, and tasks, to provide robust empirical evidence of its capabilities and limitations. This includes understanding which failure classes are amenable to this type of monitoring and which require complementary approaches.

In essence, the research seeks to constructively answer the question of how much failure detection is achievable from observable step telemetry alone, using monitors that are exceptionally cheap per step and require training only on healthy execution traces.

#### 4. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a monitoring system built on observable step telemetry, employing a one-class Echo-State Network (ESN) ensemble with CUSUM alarms, complemented by deterministic verification checks and a repair mechanism.

**4.1 Telemetry Collection:**
Each step `t` of an agent episode emits an observable vector `x_t`. This vector is constructed from three primary causal channels:
*   **Semantic Embedding (`e_t`):** A 32-dimensional hash embedding of the step's output, generated deterministically from character 3-grams without a language model.
*   **Token-Level Uncertainty (`u_t`):** Four aggregated token surprisal values.
*   **Action Metadata (`m_t`):** Includes a one-hot encoding of the action taken, log latency, output length, and error flags.
The telemetry evolves across versions:
*   **v1 (base):** 43 dimensions.
*   **v2:** Tool results are appended into the step text to ensure corrupted results influence the semantic channel.
*   **v3 (+x):** Adds 8 derived behavioral dimensions, such as cosine drift, task-anchor similarity, tool success, retry counts, latency, context ratio, depth, and self-consistency. (51 dimensions total)
*   **v4 (+g):** Incorporates 9 content-grounding dimensions, including query-result dissimilarity, result self-consistency, JSON-prefix validity, character anomaly, and a lexical flag for relevance. (60 dimensions total)
The telemetry adapter for v4 costs a median of 674 μs per step.

**4.2 Core Monitor - Echo-State Network (ESN) Ensemble with CUSUM:**
The monitor is designed as a one-class anomaly detector, trained exclusively on healthy agent runs without needing failure labels.
*   **Reservoir Computing:** For each telemetry channel `c`, a sparse, random recurrent map (`h_t^(c)`) is initialized and remains untrained. This reservoir projects the input into a high-dimensional space.
*   **Readout Layer:** A linear ridge regression readout `A^(c)` is fitted in closed form on healthy runs to predict the next step's telemetry `x_t+1^(c)` from `h_t^(c)` and `x_t^(c)`. This closed-form solution contributes to the rapid fitting time (1.7 seconds, compared to 68 seconds for a GRU).
*   **Per-Step Surprise:** The surprise `q_t^(c)` for each channel is calculated as the normalized one-step prediction error, averaged across an ensemble of `K` reservoirs. Normalization uses the residual scale `σ_err^(c)` measured on held-out healthy runs.
*   **CUSUM Accumulation:** A one-sided Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) `S_t^(c)` integrates these per-step surprises. This allows the detection of small, persistent shifts in behavior that might not trigger a threshold instantaneously.
*   **Fusion and Thresholding:** The final alarm score `s_t` is the maximum `S_t^(c)` across all channels. An alarm threshold `θ` is determined from the (1-β) quantile of maximum scores observed during healthy validation episodes, where `β` is the predefined false-alarm budget. This construction ensures the monitor is causal and operates solely on current and past observable telemetry.

**4.3 Baselines and Variants:**
The work compares the ESN-based monitor against various baselines and variants, all operating under the same one-class causal protocol:
*   Memoryless Mahalanobis and ∆-Mahalanobis detectors.
*   Cosine and self-drift, rolling entropy, linear AR models.
*   Trained sequence models like GRU, LSTM, and TCN.
*   Hybrid monitors that robust-z calibrate ESN and Mahalanobis streams, fusing them via weighting, maximum, or logistic regression.
*   A grounded stream that incorporates the content-grounding channel (`g`) using a memoryless robust-z maximum.
*   A deterministic five-line "completion check" that verifies if the final step is empty or a bare tool call.

**4.4 Deterministic Verification:**
As a complementary layer to statistical monitors, a suite of deterministic checks is introduced:
*   **`total_consistency`:** Recomputes the stated total from actual tool results received by the agent, flagging discrepancies.
*   **`required_coverage`:** Confirms that all tool calls specified as necessary by the task were made.
*   **`tool_contract`:** Validates whether a tool's returned result conforms to any of the shapes the tool is designed to return.
These checks require no healthy null, no thresholds, and no calibration, making them highly transferable.

**4.5 Repair Mechanism:**
Upon detection of a failure by either a statistical monitor or a deterministic check, the agent's trajectory is rolled back to its last fact-gathering step. The agent is then re-run live from this checkpoint with different "repair rungs" (i.e., modified prompts or instructions):
*   `resample`: Baseline control, reruns with a fresh sample.
*   `generic`: Provides a general re-check instruction.
*   `located`: Informs the agent which specific check failed, without providing values.
*   `specific`: Provides the specific finding, including problematic values.
*   `recompute`: Instructs the agent to use a calculator tool it already possesses.
*   `adaptive`: An adaptive policy.
Success is graded by an independent study oracle.

**4.6 Validation Strategy:**
The methodology emphasizes rigorous validation:
*   **Real-Ecosystem Validation:** Extensive testing on 2,823 committed agent episodes across 25 datasets, involving three frameworks (bespoke, LangGraph, AutoGen), three local agent models (qwen2.5 7b/3b, llama3.1 8b), and the commercial gemini-2.5-flash API. Telemetry is measured live.
*   **Controlled Study:** A simulator-based testbed with constructed telemetry and ground-truth onsets to study detector mechanics and evaluate performance on classes not present in real corpora.
*   **External Corpora:** Evaluation on AFTraj-2K and ATBench, benchmarks not created by the authors.
*   **Organic Failure Validation:** Assessment of monitor performance on non-injected, high-temperature (0.9) episodes with naturally occurring failures.
*   **Pre-registered Replication:** A dedicated, pre-registered study for fabrication detection, with committed design and analysis before data collection, to ensure unbiased evaluation.

#### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the capabilities and limitations of real-time LLM agent failure detection and repair:

**5.1 Monitor Performance and Characteristics:**
*   **Temporal Monitoring Efficacy:** The Echo-State Network (ESN) monitor demonstrated a detection advantage that increases with the post-onset horizon. The ESN-Mahalanobis detection gap was +0.09 for failures with ≤3 steps of post-onset horizon, increasing to +0.14 for 4–8 steps, and +0.40 for ≥9 steps. This suggests ESNs are more effective when failures develop over time.
*   **Real-Ecosystem Coverage:** On real deployments, the primary monitor achieved high detection rates for looping (0.48–1.00), tool cascade (0.17–1.00, above 0.55 on seven of nine corpora), and goal drift (0.66–0.86 on four of five corpora). However, it exhibited weak performance on content corruption classes like `wrong_document` (0.00–0.20) and `malformed_json` (0.05–0.50).
*   **Computational Efficiency:** The monitor scores a step in approximately 200 μs, and the telemetry adapter adds a median of 674 μs, keeping the total processing under one millisecond per step.
*   **Lack of Cross-Deployment Transfer:** Monitors calibrated on one deployment (e.g., qwen2.5:7b) transferred weakly to another model family (llama3.1:8b) on a matched task, resulting in near-chance AUROC (0.527) and high false alarms. Recalibration on the target deployment significantly improved performance (AUROC 0.885), indicating that per-deployment recalibration of the healthy null is mandatory.
*   **Model and API Agnosticism (with recalibration):** The approach generalized to other model families (llama3.1:8b) and a commercial API (gemini-2.5-flash), achieving AUROC 0.885 and 0.794 respectively after recalibration.
*   **Token Surprisal Irrelevance:** Ablating the token-surprisal channel showed negligible impact (+0.000 AUROC), suggesting that structured tool results are more critical than log-probability access for detection.
*   **Hybrid Monitor Utility:** A calibrated ESN+Mahalanobis hybrid, fusing streams with learned weights, provided robustness across deployment regimes. It achieved a grand-mean AUROC (0.826) that outperformed either parent alone, demonstrating its value as a default when the deployment regime is unknown.
*   **Content Blind Spot Mitigation:** The addition of a content-grounding telemetry channel (`g`) substantially improved content detection, lifting pooled content detection from 0.28 to 0.59. Specifically for `malformed_json` episodes, detection rose from 0.07 (memoryless) or 0.14 (ESN) to 0.90 for the content gate. This improvement in content detection occurred without degrading behavioral detection, which improved by +0.045.

**5.2 Organic Failures and Fabrication Detection:**
*   **Limited Transfer to Organic Failures:** Monitors calibrated on injected failures and applied to organic (non-injected) failures detected only 1 of 3 fabrications and ranked the organic failure set at/below chance (AUROC 0.31–0.42). Organic runs sampled at high temperature (0.9) also exhibited a high false-alarm rate (36%) even on healthy episodes.
*   **Deterministic Completion Check:** A simple five-line completion check caught all 7 silent aborts (failures of omission) with 0 false positives across 30 organic episodes.
*   **Fabrication via Deterministic Verifier:** A pre-registered fabrication study was underpowered, yielding too few hallucinations to score statistical monitors. However, a deterministic numeric-grounding verifier, which confirms asserted figures trace to actual tool results, successfully caught 26 of 26 provoked fabrications and 60% of overall failures (96% with `required_coverage`) with 0 false positives against the monitors' 54% recall at 17% false positives.
*   **`tool_contract` Check:** This check flagged 0 of 1825 healthy episodes while catching 46% of injected context corruption and 44% of looping failures, with 215 of 218 flagged episodes detected within one step of onset.

**5.3 Judge as Escalation Layer:**
*   **Measured Judge Performance:** A real gemini-2.5-flash judge, measured on a labeled subset, achieved a detection rate of 0.548 and a false positive rate of 0.052, which was lower detection and higher false positives than initially stipulated for escalation analysis.
*   **Complementary Strengths:** The judge and monitors demonstrated complementary failure modes: the judge was perfect on goal drift but nearly blind to context corruption (0.18), whereas the monitor showed the reverse pattern (1.00 on context corruption).
*   **Call Savings vs. Detection Recovery:** The escalation architecture maintained call savings (6-8% of judge-every-step calls) but, with measured judge rates, reduced detection recovery from a simulated 82% to 43%.

**5.4 Repair Mechanism Effectiveness:**
*   **Significant Recovery:** Rolling back and re-running flagged episodes recovered 45% of failures using the "located" repair rung, compared to a 16% recovery rate from a plain resampling control (p = 0.0005). This intervention lifted net task success from 52% to 73% for approximately one extra model call per run.
*   **Effective Repair Rung:** The "located" rung, which informs the agent *which check failed* but provides no specific values, proved most effective. Conversely, providing the agent with the specific finding or instructing it to recompute did not significantly outperform simple retry luck.
*   **Partial Coverage of Repair:** While behavioral alarms were consistently followed by repair attempts, only goal drift was reliably fixed by retrying (4 of 5 cases). Failures like `tool_cascade` and `looping` typically escalated (ending the episode quickly) rather than recovering, as the underlying tool layer was broken.

#### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of LLM agent reliability by presenting a cost-effective and practical framework for real-time failure detection and repair. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

*   **Cost-Effective Agent Monitoring:** The core contribution is a demonstration that a substantial portion of LLM agent failures can be detected using lightweight, microsecond-scale monitors that analyze observable telemetry. This offers a significantly cheaper alternative to auditing every step with a second, expensive LLM, making reliable agent deployment more economically feasible.
*   **Enhanced Agent Robustness and Reliability:** By combining statistical anomaly detection with deterministic verification checks and a reactive repair mechanism, the system addresses a broad spectrum of agent failure modes. This multi-layered approach provides a more comprehensive defense against common issues like looping, tool errors, goal drift, and data corruption, thereby increasing the overall robustness of LLM agents.
*   **Improved Task Success Rates:** The ability to detect failures mid-episode and initiate targeted repair via rollback and re-execution led to a notable increase in net task success, from 52% to 73%. This direct improvement in performance can have practical implications for applications relying on autonomous LLM agents.
*   **Actionable Insights for Agent Developers:** The findings highlight the importance of specific telemetry channels (e.g., content-grounding) for addressing blind spots and underscore the necessity of per-deployment recalibration for statistical monitors. These insights can guide future development of more robust and context-aware agent monitoring systems.
*   **Guidance for Intervention Strategies:** The repair mechanism findings suggest that providing agents with information about *which check failed* is more effective for recovery than providing specific values or recomputation instructions. This provides practical guidance for designing more effective self-correction and human-in-the-loop repair strategies.
*   **Broad Applicability:** The validation across multiple agent frameworks, local models, and a commercial API (Gemini-2.5-flash) indicates that the proposed methods are not tied to a specific LLM or ecosystem, suggesting broad applicability in various real-world deployment scenarios.
*   **Transparency and Reproducibility:** The public release of code, traces, and results, along with detailed provenance mapping, contributes to transparency and reproducibility, allowing other researchers to build upon and verify the findings.

In conclusion, this work offers a pragmatic approach to enhancing the reliability of LLM agents by providing a low-cost, real-time "first line of defense," complemented by targeted deterministic checks and an effective repair mechanism. This framework addresses critical operational challenges in the rapidly evolving landscape of LLM agent deployment.
