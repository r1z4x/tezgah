## Claim, with its exact numbers

An audit of accepted camera-ready papers at ICLR, ICML, NeurIPS and USENIX
Security, on a deliberately conservative definition of "hallucinated citation":

- **Reference-level rates are usually below one percent** - so the failure is
  invisible per reference and visible per proceedings.
- **In 2025, roughly one in twenty accepted NeurIPS and USENIX Security papers
  contains at least two likely hallucinated academic-paper-like references** under
  the strict definition.
- Post-ChatGPT increases in several venue families, with "a visible tail of papers
  carr[ying] several (5+) hallucinated citations in a single bibliography", and
  cases among award-winning papers.

The authors' conclusion is the actionable half: "citation integrity is not
reliably enforced by peer review alone, yet the problem is tractable" - the check
they ship is mechanical (`refchecker`).

## Why it matters here

The layer's own rule is the right one ("Never write a citation from memory.
Verify each one against two of Semantic Scholar, CrossRef, arXiv or OpenAlex"),
and E1's P8 measures its enforcement at zero: a literature note with no
identifier, no URL and a nonexistent source passes `check`. This paper supplies
the rate the rule is defending against, at the venue quality level the repository
aspires to, and the shape of the countermeasure (a mechanical check, not a
reviewer's attention).
