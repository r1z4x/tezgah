# Agent Safety Should Be a Runtime Contract

- id: alphaXiv 2608.11274 (arXiv preprint), 2026-08-11
- authors: Albus W. Ng, Yi Han, Jusheng Zhang, Wenhao Wang (Vast Intelligence Lab; Southwest University; Sun Yat-sen University)
- url: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11274
- read: alphaXiv report, 2026-09-19

## Claim

Model-level alignment (RLHF, DPO, Constitutional AI) is structurally insufficient
for agents that execute code, write files and send messages. Production safety
lives in a **runtime contract** with two faces, joined by compositional gating:

- **preventive** - block a dangerous action before it runs (sandboxes, permission
  gates, output filters, trajectory monitors);
- **evidential** - require verifiable proof that intended safe actions happened,
  *gating task submission on hard evidence* (test runs, log captures, file diffs,
  citation grounding).

Five mismatches they name as the motivation: statistical proxy vs. formal
specification; training distribution vs. open-world deployment; unverifiable
inner monologue vs. replayable trajectory; plausible output vs. grounded
citation; and single-layer defence.

Mechanism taxonomy: **preventive / detective / corrective / structural**, with
corrective = "triggered after detection: human-in-the-loop escalation, automatic
rollback". Design principles are adapted from Saltzer-Schroeder (fail-safe
defaults, complete mediation, least privilege, separation of privilege,
economy of mechanism).
