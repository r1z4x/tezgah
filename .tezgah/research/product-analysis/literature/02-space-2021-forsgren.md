# SPACE: developer productivity is not one number

- **Source:** Nicole Forsgren, Margaret-Anne Storey, Chandra Maddila, Tom Zimmermann,
  Brian Houck, Jenna Butler, "The SPACE of Developer Productivity: There's more to it
  than you think", *ACM Queue* 19(1), February 2021, pp. 20-48.
- **Read:** the Microsoft Research publication record,
  `https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/`
  (fetched 2026-09-19), plus the OpenAlex record `10.1145/3454122.3454124`
  (`orx discover openalex`, 2026-09-19, 158 citations). Both agree on authors, venue,
  volume and pages.

## What the source says (verbatim abstract)

> "Developer productivity is about more than an individual's activity levels or the
> efficiency of the engineering systems relied on to ship software, and it cannot be
> measured by a single metric or dimension. The SPACE framework captures different
> dimensions of productivity, and here we demonstrate how this framework can be used
> to understand productivity in practice and why using it will help teams better
> understand developer productivity and create better measures to inform their work
> and teams."

The 2025 DORA report's companion insight names SPACE as one of the frameworks that
"can help you identify a constellation of metrics to understand and influence
developer productivity", alongside Google's SEQ and HEART (see
`03-dora-2025.md`).

## What this settles for the structure

- A single productivity or quality number is not admissible evidence on its own; the
  PE axis must report a constellation and say which dimensions it did and did not
  cover. That is the abstract's own claim, and the reason the scorecard below scores
  coverage rather than averaging a single index.

## What this source does NOT support

- It does not name which dimensions matter for a given team, and it does not license
  "measure everything". The abstract explicitly frames the framework as a way to
  *choose* better measures.
