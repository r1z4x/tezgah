# H3 - three samples of the writer's prose before the edit

**What ran.** `harness/run.py --track h3`, transport `host`. Both arms edited the six Turkish
fixture drafts under the shipped contract; arm B was additionally given the three writer
samples in `harness/voice.json`. Judging was a forced choice per judge and per order: which
edit sounds more like that writer.

**What the rows say.** Arm B took **0.67** of the twelve judgements (0.5 on three items, 0.75
on two, 1.0 on one), above the 0.6 the protocol predicted and above the 0.5 falsifier, but
with three of six items at exactly 0.5 and no item preferring arm A outright.

**Which claim it moves.** C05, supported with the margin named: passing the threshold on
twelve judgements from two judge tiers is weak evidence, and the falsifier sits one item away.

**What it does not show.** The samples are a fixture I wrote, so "more like this writer" means
"more like a plain first-person voice I authored", not "more like the user". The
post-editing literature this rests on found that perceived authenticity can rise while the
text stays measurably closer to model output - so a forced-choice win here is not evidence
that machine traces are gone.
