# Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM Text for Personal Style (2604.24444)

**What it is.** A pre-registered online study (n=81) where participants post-edit
LLM drafts for tasks where personal style matters to them.

**What it claims.** Post-editing *increases* embedding-measured stylistic similarity
to the participant's own unassisted writing and reduces similarity to raw LLM
output - but the result still stays stylistically closer to LLM text than to the
participant's control writing, and shows *less* stylistic diversity than unassisted
human text. A gap opens between perceived authenticity and measured similarity:
readers often take post-edited text as the person's own style even while model
stylistic traces remain.

**What it gives us.** Three things: voice capture from the writer's own samples has a
measurable direction; the measurement can be embedding similarity to the writer's
own corpus (not an AI detector); and the honest ceiling - an edit can win on
perceived authenticity while still failing a stylometric comparison, so a claim about
"no AI traces" is not supportable by a preference judgement alone.

**How it would change the skill.** Make the writer's own samples a required input when
they exist (not an optional nicety), and state the ceiling in the skill so an edit is
never sold as undetectable.
