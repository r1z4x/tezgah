## What it is
Agentic Harness Engineering closes a loop that edits a coding agent's harness automatically.
It names three matched observability pillars: *component* observability gives every editable
harness component a file-level representation, *experience* observability distills raw
trajectories into a layered evidence corpus, and *decision* observability pairs every edit with
a self-declared prediction that is later checked against the next round's outcomes - which is
what lets an edit be falsified rather than merely kept.

The reported result: ten iterations lift pass@1 on Terminal-Bench 2 from 69.7% to 77.0%,
above the human-designed Codex-CLI harness (71.9%) and above the ACE and TF-GRPO baselines.
The frozen harness transfers without further evolution - SWE-bench-Verified at 12% fewer tokens
than the seed, and +5.1 to +10.1pp across three other model families on Terminal-Bench 2.
The ablation is the part this line needs: the gain localizes to tools, middleware and long-term
memory, **not** the system prompt, so factual structure transfers where prose-level strategy
does not.

## Why it is in this line
The boundary question asks which layer tezgah sits in. AHE is the source that names, as an
object of engineering, the layer **between the model and the task**: the components that
mediate tools and execution. Its file-level component representation and its "one edit, one
prediction" pairing are the design a harness layer can be recognized by, and its ablation says
the load-bearing half of that layer is mechanical structure rather than injected prose - which
is exactly the distinction the page has to draw between tezgah's layer and the host it wraps.
