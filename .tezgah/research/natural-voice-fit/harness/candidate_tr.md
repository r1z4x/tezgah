## Turkish drafts

Applies when the draft is Turkish (`tr`). It is a set of sourced editorial
defects with a replacement for each, not a list of tells that prove a machine
wrote the text: no primary Turkish source calls these constructions machine
text, and the skill must not claim it.

**Replace the calque, keep the term.** Word-for-word translation is the thing
readers call "smelling of translation". Replace it where a Turkish equivalent is
established, and leave a loan alone where the register has adopted it.

- `organize etmek` -> `düzenlemek`; `realize etmek` -> `gerçekleştirmek`
  (the dictionary itself redirects `realizasyon` to `gerçekleştirme`);
  `implemente etmek` -> `uygulamak`, `hayata geçirmek` (no dictionary entry);
  `aksiyon almak` -> `önlem almak`, `harekete geçmek`; `insanlara dokunmak` ->
  `temas kurmak`; `fokuslanmak` -> `odaklanmak`; `vizyoner` -> `uzak görüşlü`.
- In the corporate register some English loans are the established term and
  speakers give "no exact Turkish equivalent" as their reason for using them
  (`deadline`, `brief`, `feedback`). Leave those; a blanket ban reads wrong to
  the reader it is written for.

**Cut the bureaucratic imitation.** `-mektedir / -maktadır` chains, `-dır/-dir`
copulas stacked on every sentence, and impersonal nominalisation imitate the
official-correspondence register, which is a real convention for official
letters and the wrong voice everywhere else. Prefer the simple present or
`-yor`.

**Cut the filler the newsroom guide names.** `olmak`, `yapmak`, `etmek` used as
support verbs where a verb exists; `olan`, `olup`; `tane`, `adet` as counters
("insanlar da tane ile sayılmazlar").

**Cut the redundancy the usage guide names.** A third-person pronoun that only
echoes the conjugation (`onun`, `onların`); a pronoun placed before the noun it
refers to; `ki` stacked on an explanatory clause that already says it; `olarak`
glued to an adjective that needs no adverb; `altında` where `döneminde`,
`koşullarında` or `yönetiminde` is meant; the double passive
(`görülebilineceği unutulmamalıdır` -> `görülebileceği`).

**Cut the connective padding.** `söz konusu`, `bu bağlamda`, `bu kapsamda`,
`ayrıca belirtmek gerekir ki`, `unutulmamalıdır ki`: say the thing, or link the
two clauses with the word that does the linking (`bu nedenle`, `oysa`, `ama`).
Do not stack two contrastive conjunctions in one sentence.

**Do not touch inverted sentences.** `Devrik cümle` is a sentence whose
predicate has moved, not a defective one: the standard Turkish usage guide says
so explicitly, and the one quantitative study finds inversion to be a register
feature that is more common in writers on social subjects. What is defective is
a broken link between subject and predicate, and that is a coherence problem,
not a word-order rule.

**Do not invent ceilings.** There is no verified Turkish sentence-length or
paragraph-length limit; a number here would be made up. Measure before you
assert one.

**Address form and punctuation.** `siz` is the default; `sen` belongs to young
audiences and marketing taglines. Do not mix the polite `-(y)InIz` verb form
into ordinary prose - the Turkish style guide rejects it as authoritative
tone. Do not use the em dash for emphasis in Turkish; the style guide rules it
out.
