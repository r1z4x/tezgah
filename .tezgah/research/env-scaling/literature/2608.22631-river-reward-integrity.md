# 2608.22631 — Learning Generalizable Behaviors for Terminal Agents (RIVER)

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22631 — Yihang Yao, Bo Pang, Xuan Phi Nguyen, Ding Zhao, Shafiq
Joty, Semih Yavuz (Salesforce AI Research; CMU) — 2026-08-23, cs.LG. Report read in full; the
arXiv abstract was fetched as the second record.

## What it is
The counter-argument in its sharpest form - a claim about what the reward signal, not the
environment supply, is doing. Its "Agentic Compositional Generalization hypothesis" says
pre-training and SFT supply low-level single-turn skills while RL "primarily shapes high-level
decision-making behaviors", which makes the verifier - "which determines which behaviors are
reinforced" - "more important than simply increasing environment quantity or diversity"
(abstract). The RIVER recipe follows from that: filter environments by eight quality rubrics
(VERIFIER-TOO-WEAK, INSTR-VERIFIER-MISMATCH, ANSWER-LEAK, TASK-TRIVIAL and four more), drop
environments an oracle solves pass@k=0, then add a turn-wise behaviour penalty.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- The yield of the quality filter, which is the number that carries the argument: "over 60% of
  environments exhibited at least one quality issue, leaving fewer than 40% suitable".
- Selection beats scale, stated in the abstract: "Using fewer than 30% of the TMax training
  environments, River improves RL gains by 106% and 30% on average for models ranging from 2B to
  27B on Terminal-Bench-Lite and Terminal-Bench-v2.1, respectively."
- Benchmarks: the RIVER-8B model "achieved the highest average performance (19.4%) across the
  four terminal benchmarks", against "OpenThinker-8B-RL (17.8%) and TMax-RL-8B (14.6%)".
- The behaviour penalty is a repeated-turn rule: "A turn is flagged as repetitive if its command
  and observation have a Jaccard similarity greater than 0.8 with an earlier turn."

## Why it is in this line
It is one of the required counter-argument sources, and the one whose measurement is closest to
a *task-quality* claim rather than an *environment-count* claim: more than 60% of a large
synthetic environment set carries at least one checkable defect. For a repository whose corpus
is hand-written and whose checks are hidden, that number is the outside estimate of how much of
a corpus can be quietly wrong - and its remedy (filter by rubrics, then by an oracle) is
available without a training loop. Its repeated-turn penalty is also the only mechanism in this
set that is a harness clause rather than a weight update.

## Quality and limits
Formal: arXiv preprint, Salesforce AI Research and CMU, full author list, no venue or peer review
visible in the record read. Every number is the authors' own, cited and not reproduced here. The
60%/40% split is measured on the TMax collection by this paper's own rubrics, so it is a
statement about that collection under that filter, not a general rate.
