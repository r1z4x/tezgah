# 2608.04934 — State2State: Environment-Derived Mid-Training for LLM Agents

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04934 — Xuanyu Lei, Yiqi Zhu, Chenliang Li, Kaiming Liu, Peng
Li, Ming Yan, Jieping Ye, Ya-Qin Zhang, Yang Liu (Tsinghua AIR; Alibaba) — 2026-08-05,
cs.CL / cs.LG. Report read in full; the arXiv abstract was fetched as the second record; code at
https://github.com/THUNLP-MT/State2State.

## What it is
The branch that removes the task as well as the environment: an "environment learning" paradigm
where a policy acquires skills "solely through environment interaction, without externally
specified tasks". State2State explores the environment, keeps reachable states that can be
reached reproducibly, and turns each into a task - "reach this target observation" - whose
verifier is the observation match itself, so no handcrafted reward function is needed. It trains
with GRPO plus dynamic sampling (only rollout groups with mixed outcomes are kept) and serves as
a mid-training stage before ordinary RL on human-specified tasks.

Numbers, the authors' own:
- Datasets built: "The generated datasets for ScienceWorld and ALFWorld contain 8,000 and 4,256
  tasks, respectively, characterized by unique observations and varied action sequences".
- Environments: ALFWorld and ScienceWorld (text-based), with an extension to MobileWorld (GUI);
  training by `verl`/`vllm`, learning rate 1e-6, KL coefficient 0.01, GRPO group size 8, ~80
  mid-training steps.
- Reported outcome, in the authors' wording: "Experiments on ALFWorld and ScienceWorld show that
  State2State improves agent performance as a standalone environment-learning stage in most
  settings. As initialization for downstream RL, it further improves final performance and
  learning efficiency, with promising evidence of cross-environment generalization" (abstract).
  The report states the same result qualitatively and does not give a headline percentage in the
  section read.

## Why it is in this line
It is the extreme of the environment-first axis and the cleanest statement of what the axis
buys when it works: the environment supplies both the task distribution *and* the verifier, at
zero human labelling cost. It also marks the other boundary: even here the implementation is a
policy training loop with rollouts and a group-relative gradient, and the verifier is an exact
state match - a property only reproducible sandboxes have. Nothing in it is available to a layer
that owns no weights.
