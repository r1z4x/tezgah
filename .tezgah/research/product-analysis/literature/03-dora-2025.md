# DORA 2025: AI is an amplifier, and verification is the tax

- **Source:** DORA, *State of AI-assisted Software Development*, 2025 report, plus the
  companion insight "Balancing AI tensions: Moving from AI adoption to effective SDLC
  use" (Jessica Baolin, Nathen Harvey, 2026-03-10).
- **Read:** `https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/` and
  `https://dora.dev/insights/balancing-ai-tensions/` (both fetched 2026-09-19).
  Survey window and the qualitative sample are as stated on those two pages.

## Findings used here (verbatim where quoted)

- "The State of AI-assisted Software Development report reveals AI's primary role is
  as an amplifier, magnifying an organization's existing strengths and weaknesses.
  The greatest returns on AI investment come not from the tools themselves, but from
  a strategic focus on the underlying organizational system."
- "90% of technology professionals now use AI at work, and over 80% believe it has
  increased their productivity."
- "higher AI adoption is associated with an increase in both software delivery
  throughput and software delivery instability."
- "30% of developers currently report little to no trust in the code generated by AI.
  Because AI tools are currently incapable of signaling uncertainty and output
  hallucinations with high confidence, engineers are forced to treat every
  interaction as potentially deceptive."
- "the time saved in creation is frequently re-allocated to auditing and
  verification"; "the time saved during initial code or content generation is often
  re-allocated to verification overhead".
- Method for the qualitative half: "a thematic deep dive into 1,110 open-ended survey
  responses from Google software engineers in Q3 2025".
- Their prescribed countermeasures: "Measure impact, not output" (naming Google's
  speed/ease/quality, SPACE, HEART, Value Stream Management), shift review left to
  the author, work in small batches, "Enable AI-accessible internal data", reduce tool
  sprawl.

## What this settles for the structure

1. The amplifier claim is the load-bearing one for this research line: an agent
   harness does not fix a weak analysis habit, it multiplies it. A weak research
   routing path stays weak and gets cheaper to produce at volume.
2. The failure mode to design against is named by the report: unverifiable output
   shifted onto a human reviewer as a verification tax. The counter is evidence the
   reader can check without re-deriving it - cited sources, not prose.
3. "Measure impact, not output" is the same rule HEART states for metrics, applied to
   the development process.
4. Association is not causation in the throughput/instability sentence, and the
   report says "associated with". Any claim built on it must keep that verb.

## What this source does NOT support

- It does not measure tezgah, this repository, or any particular prompt-routing
  change. It is evidence about the class of change, not about this one.
- The percentages are survey self-report inside one company's engineers plus a global
  survey; they are not a controlled measurement of a causal effect.
