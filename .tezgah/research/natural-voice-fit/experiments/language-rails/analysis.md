# H5 - the same rail design in a second language

**What ran.** `harness/run.py --track h5`, transport `host`, four German fixture drafts (two
executive, two developer). Arm A carried the shipped English contract; arm B carried it plus
`harness/candidate_de.md`, written the way the rail requires: the German orthographic
authority, the DWDS corpus, the measured anglicism evidence, and the German plain-language
supplement to ISO 24495-1.

**What the rows say.** Mean native-German delta **+1.13**, 95% CI **[+0.38, +1.63]**, three of
four items clearly positive and one at zero. Marker density per 100 words fell from **1.98**
to **1.01**. Under the strictest reading - that the language axis needs a rail rather than a
translated Turkish rule set - the German instance is the larger effect of the two, and it is
the one language in this line whose rail was assembled from an authority and a corpus without
an in-language translationese study of its own.

**Which claim it moves.** C04, supported: the contract text changes behaviour in a second
language when the section is written for that language, at least as much as in the first.

**What it does not show.** Four fixture items, one generator, two cheap judge tiers, and no
human German reader; the CI is wide enough to include the +0.5 threshold and a much larger
effect. The German section was written by the same session that wrote the Turkish one, so a
shared style of instruction is confounded with the language. It says nothing about languages
whose rails are missing a leg - which the coverage note measures, and which the report names
as the design's boundary.
