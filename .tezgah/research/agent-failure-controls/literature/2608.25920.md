#### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems (MASs) are increasingly being applied to complex, long-horizon tasks such as web interaction and software development. While MASs offer capabilities for distributed planning, information gathering, tool use, execution, and verification, their reliability remains a significant challenge for real-world deployment. The broader research landscape in MAS reliability has focused on developing benchmarks, failure diagnosis methods, and execution repair strategies.

Existing diagnostic studies involve collecting execution trajectories, constructing failure taxonomies, and localizing errors to specific agents or steps. Repair methods typically employ techniques such as rerunning entire executions, self-reflection, critic feedback, or trajectory-level guidance to generate new attempts at a task. However, the paper identifies two primary limitations in the current state of MAS debugging and repair. First, complete reruns involve resampling upstream model decisions, which complicates the attribution of terminal success to a specific repair. It becomes unclear whether a successful rerun genuinely fixed the original failure or merely circumvented it through stochastic variation in LLM outputs. Second, task-level failure verdicts and automatically assigned categories may not pinpoint the precise, trace-localized behavior that requires intervention. This lack of precise control and attribution hinders a fundamental understanding of whether current methods causally repair MAS failures or primarily rely on the inherent randomness of LLM sampling for stochastic recovery. This research addresses this gap by introducing a controlled evaluation framework and a detailed failure dataset to systematically investigate the effectiveness and causal nature of MAS repair mechanisms.

#### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to evaluate the effectiveness of LLM multi-agent system repair methods by distinguishing between causal repair and stochastic recovery. The authors aim to answer a fundamental question: do existing debugging and repair methods genuinely address MAS failures, or do they primarily achieve success by leveraging the inherent randomness of LLM sampling during repeated executions?

The motivation for this study stems from the observed reliability bottlenecks in deploying LLM-based MASs for complex tasks. As these systems move into more consequential applications, understanding and systematically addressing their failures is critical. Current approaches often involve rerunning entire execution trajectories, which can obscure the true impact of a repair strategy. If a system succeeds on a retry, it is difficult to determine whether a specific intervention was effective or if random variations in LLM outputs simply led to a different, successful path.

To achieve their objective, the authors establish several key goals:
1.  **Develop a Controlled Evaluation Framework:** Design and implement SymTrace, a replay-oriented logging system capable of recording MAS execution trajectories, establishing intervention anchors, and reconstructing execution prefixes. This framework aims to enable reliable reproduction of MAS failures and isolate the effects of specific interventions without resampling prior execution steps.
2.  **Construct a Comprehensive Failure Dataset:** Create SymFail, a human-annotated dataset of MAS failure trajectories. This dataset is intended to provide detailed information, including graph-linked failure locations, symptom categories, and trace evidence, serving as a foundation for systematic debugging studies.
3.  **Conduct an Empirical Study:** Perform a large-scale empirical study across multiple mainstream MAS frameworks to evaluate the reliability of existing failure reproduction and repair methods under controlled conditions.
4.  **Explore Symptom-Driven Intervention:** Investigate the effectiveness of a novel symptom-driven intervention method that leverages localized evidence for targeted repair.
5.  **Facilitate Future Research:** Release the SymTrace framework, SymFail dataset, and experimental results to support reproducibility and further research in MAS debugging and repair.

These objectives are motivated by the need for actionable insights into MAS debugging, aiming to move beyond anecdotal observations of success and towards a robust understanding of how to systematically improve the reliability of multi-agent systems.

#### 4. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces SymTrace, a replay-oriented logging system, and SymFail, a human-annotated failure-trajectory dataset, to systematically evaluate MAS failure reproduction and repair.

**4.1 SymTrace System Design**
SymTrace operates in two modes: Snapshot Mode and Replay Mode.

*   **Snapshot Mode:** This mode captures a native MAS execution and transforms it into a structured trajectory for later replay.
    *   **Boundary Logging:** During native execution, framework-specific hooks intercept LLM request-response pairs and tool call-observation pairs. SymTrace records the request, the realized result, and the event's position in the execution, preserving non-deterministic values.
    *   **Trace Construction:** The recorded events are organized into an event-dependency graph (G) with edges representing data or control dependencies. The observed event order (O) is also recorded, as the graph may allow multiple valid schedules.
    *   **Replay Bundle:** The recorded values (R), execution structure (G, O), task (T), initial state (s₀), and runtime configuration (c) are combined into a replay bundle (S).

*   **Replay Mode:** This mode takes a replay bundle and an intervention target to reconstruct and resume execution.
    *   **Result Injection:** The native MAS is restarted using T, s₀, and c. When the MAS reaches a model or tool boundary *before* the designated intervention target, SymTrace retrieves the corresponding historical record from R instead of invoking the live endpoint.
    *   **Boundary Matching:** The intercepted call is validated against the expected record using its event position and canonicalized request content. A mismatch terminates replay, while a successful match allows the recorded result to be returned to the MAS. This process reconstructs the target prefix, with content hashes validating reused prefix nodes.
    *   **Live Resume:** Once the verified prefix reaches the designated target, SymTrace stops returning historical results and applies the specified intervention. Model and tool interactions revert to live endpoints, and execution continues from the intervention point, generating a new downstream trajectory.
    *   **Replay Scope:** This defines that the prefix before the target is strictly reconstructed from recorded results, preserving the logical history. The execution from the intervention onward is live and may diverge.

**4.2 SymFail Dataset Construction**
SymFail is a dataset of human-annotated failure trajectories designed for MAS debugging studies.
*   **Candidate Selection:** The dataset is built upon 200 tasks from WebArena-Verified Hard and AssistantBench, known for multi-step web tasks with verifiable outcomes.
*   **Trajectory Collection:** Each task was executed once with three representative MAS frameworks: AG2, CrewAI, and Magentic-One, yielding 600 initial trajectories. Of these, 536 evaluator-confirmed failures with complete traces and graph artifacts were retained.
*   **Failure Annotation:**
    *   Four non-exclusive, trace-observable symptom categories (C1-C4) were derived from an existing Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy: C1 (Implicated constraint and conflicting behavior), C2 (Repeated history and unfinished objective), C3 (Unresolved runtime condition and its evidence), and C4 (Explicit plan, action, and outcome inconsistency).
    *   Four annotators with software engineering backgrounds underwent calibration. Three independently assigned all applicable categories, selected a primary category, and identified the earliest trace-supported actionable node with evidence and rationale. A fourth annotator reviewed all inputs to produce the final, adjudicated annotation.
    *   Inter-annotator reliability was measured using Fleiss’ κ (0.62 for primary category, 0.81 for failure-node type) and exact node agreement (73.88% among all three, 95.90% for at least two).

**4.3 Study Setup and Methods**
*   **Study Scope:** The study focused on the 536 SymFail failures.
*   **Baselines:** Three task-level debugging and repair methods were compared, each allowed up to three complete attempts:
    *   **Unguided Full Rerun:** Restarts MAS from original task without guidance.
    *   **Self-Reflection:** Provides MAS with its failed final output and asks it to reflect before re-solving.
    *   **Critic-Agent:** Employs a separate critic agent to review failed output and provide corrective guidance.
*   **Proposed Method:** **Suspicious-Node Intervention** is a node-level repair method. It treats a trace node as an intervention anchor if its local behavior exhibits observable C1-C4 symptoms. Deterministic rules and a semantic judge evaluate nodes. If a suspicion score exceeds a threshold, SymTrace reconstructs the prefix, applies a repair instruction conditioned on the judge’s classification, and resumes live execution from that point (single intervention).
*   **Node-Level Controls:** Random-Node Intervention and Last-Node Intervention, which receive a single selective-replay opportunity without symptom evidence.
*   **Evaluation Metrics:** Failure reproduction rate (rep_k) and repair success rate (pass@k) were used.
*   **Model and Efficiency:** Deepseek-v4-flash (temperature 0.00) was used for all experiments. API calls per repair were also reported.
*   **Statistical Analysis:** 95% Wilson confidence intervals, 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for rate differences, two-sided exact McNemar tests, and Holm correction for family-wise error rates were applied.

#### 5. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the reliability of MAS failure reproduction and repair across three MAS frameworks (AG2, CrewAI, Magentic-One) and the 536 human-annotated failures in SymFail.

**5.1 RQ1: How Reliably Can MAS Failures Be Reproduced?**
The results indicate that SymTrace Replay provides a consistent advantage over Unguided Full Rerun in reproducing failures.
*   **Per-Execution Reproduction (rep_1):** SymTrace Replay achieved an 80.78% reproduction rate, compared to 67.97% for Unguided Full Rerun.
*   **Consistent Reproduction Across Three Executions (rep_3):** SymTrace Replay demonstrated a 52.43% consistent reproduction rate, while Unguided Full Rerun achieved 41.42%.
*   **Prefix Exactness:** SymTrace achieved 100.00% prefix exactness, meaning all reconstructed prefix nodes passed content-hash validation. This indicates that SymTrace accurately reproduces the execution history before an intervention point.
*   **Impact of Prefix Length:** The reproduction advantage of SymTrace increased with the length of the reused prefix. For 2-3 reused nodes, the gain in rep_1 and rep_3 was 9.80 and 6.82 percentage points, respectively. These gains rose to 17.76 and 18.69 points for 4-8 reused nodes, and 25.69 and 27.08 points for nine or more reused nodes.
*   **Sources of Remaining Variation:** The gap between perfect prefix reconstruction and end-to-end reproduction was attributed to factors such as short replay prefixes, post-target execution variation (where live execution resumes), and potential measurement uncertainty from the LLM-as-a-judge pipeline.

**5.2 RQ2: Can Task-Level Re-execution Reliably Repair Failed MAS Executions?**
The study's findings suggest that task-level repair methods primarily rely on stochastic regeneration rather than targeted failure-specific repair.
*   **Low Repair Rates:** Existing unguided rerun methods exhibited low repair rates. Unguided Full Rerun achieved a pass@3 rate of 6.90% across all failures.
*   **Ineffectiveness of Feedback-Based Methods:** Generic reflection (Self-Reflection, 4.29% pass@3) and critique (Critic-Agent, 3.73% pass@3) methods did not provide a consistent repair advantage over Unguided Full Rerun. Unguided Full Rerun performed comparably or better across all MAS frameworks and failure categories. This suggests that the additional feedback provided by these methods did not reliably target the source failure mechanism under the given budget.
*   **Outcome Instability:** Complete re-execution demonstrated outcome instability. An analysis of 54 initially successful executions showed that 85 out of 162 subsequent attempts (three per case) resulted in failures, with 39 of the 54 source cases regressing at least once. This indicates that stochasticity can destabilize successful outcomes as well as occasionally "repair" failed ones without addressing the root cause.
*   **Case Analysis:** A specific CrewAI case highlighted that an evaluator-accepted answer could be produced without resolving the source failure, relying on general historical patterns rather than the required evidence that was unavailable to the MAS.

**5.3 RQ3: Are Node-Level Symptom Signals Actionable for Targeted Repair?**
Node-level symptom signals were found to provide actionable evidence for targeted repair, significantly outperforming task-level and other node-level baselines.
*   **Superior Repair Rate:** Suspicious-Node Intervention successfully repaired 20.15% of the failed cases with a single intervention. This represents a 191.89% improvement over the strongest task-level baseline (Unguided Full Rerun, 6.90% pass@3).
*   **Outperformance of Node-Level Controls:** Suspicious-Node Intervention also substantially outperformed other node-level controls that used a single selective-replay opportunity but lacked symptom evidence: Random-Node Intervention (3.73%) and Last-Node Intervention (1.31%). This suggests that selective replay alone is not sufficient; symptom-based targeting and guidance are crucial.
*   **Cross-MAS Consistency:** The method ranking remained stable across all three MAS architectures, with Suspicious-Node Intervention performing best on AG2 (16.37%), CrewAI (25.00%), and Magentic-One (18.78%). This indicates that localized symptom evidence provides a more consistent repair signal than repeated full-trajectory regeneration.
*   **Efficiency:** Suspicious-Node Intervention also achieved the highest number of accepted repairs per 1,000 API calls and the lowest number of API calls per repair across all MASs, suggesting greater repair efficiency.

#### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This study contributes to the field of LLM multi-agent systems by fundamentally distinguishing between two forms of failure resolution: genuinely correcting a recorded MAS failure versus achieving stochastic recovery through a different execution path. The introduction of **SymTrace**, a controlled evaluation framework, is significant because it allows researchers to replay specific execution prefixes and apply interventions at designated points, thereby enabling a more rigorous assessment of repair mechanisms by isolating the impact of changes. This moves beyond traditional evaluations that may conflate intervention effectiveness with the inherent variability of LLM outputs.

The creation of **SymFail**, a human-annotated dataset of 536 failure trajectories with detailed localization and symptom categorization, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset supports systematic study, benchmarking, and the development of more precise debugging tools for MASs.

The empirical findings have several implications. The observation that existing unguided rerun methods are unreliable and that task-level feedback mechanisms (like self-reflection or critic agents) do not consistently outperform unguided reruns under equal budgets suggests that simply retrying or providing general feedback may not address the underlying failure mechanisms. This highlights the limitations of current high-level repair strategies.

Conversely, the success of **Suspicious-Node Intervention**, which achieved a 191.89% improvement over the strongest baseline with a single intervention, underscores the potential of symptom-driven, localized repair. This approach demonstrates that precise execution control and actionable, trace-localized symptom signals are more effective than stochastic regeneration across diverse MAS architectures. This finding can guide future research and development towards more targeted and efficient debugging strategies for MASs.

The research provides actionable insights for improving MAS debugging and repair, advocating for methods that preserve execution history before intervention, localize the intended change, and rigorously assess whether the specific failure mechanism was corrected. Ultimately, these contributions aim to pave the way for more robust and reliable deployment of multi-agent systems in complex, real-world applications by offering a methodology for understanding and addressing their failures more systematically.

**Potential Future Directions:**
*   **Data Coverage Expansion:** Future work could extend SymFail to include a wider diversity of real-world failure trajectories, additional MAS structures, varying interaction patterns, and new task domains beyond the current benchmark-derived tasks.
*   **Automated Evaluation Refinement:** The study used LLM judges for failure reproduction. Future efforts could focus on enhancing the accuracy of these judges by incorporating expert verification for ambiguous or low-confidence cases and developing hybrid human-LLM annotation approaches.
*   **Model Coverage:** Repeating the evaluation across a broader range of LLM models, providers, scales, and future versions could help determine the generality of the observed reproduction and repair patterns across different underlying language models.
