# 2609.04128 — Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents

arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04128 — Zhiyuan Fan, Tinghao Yu, Yuanjun Cai, Jiang Zhou,
Jiangtao Guan, Jincheng Liu, Yun Yang, Dingxin Hu, Zhuo Han, Xing Wu, Feng Zhang, Lilin Wang
(Hunyuan Team, Tencent) — 2026-09-03, cs.AI. Report read in full; the arXiv abstract was
fetched as the second record.

## What it is
The 2026 successor to the generation pipelines, and the source that states the scaling premise
in one sentence: **"Scaling interactive and verifiable environments is critical for training
terminal agents."** It then names the failure of the naive version of that premise: as frontier
models get stronger, "environments synthesized from scratch become less challenging and thus
provide limited learning signals", and environments that stay easy "are often discarded,
leading to wasted resources and reduced training diversity".

Its answer is *environment evolution*: increase environment difficulty off-policy, generation
by generation, along three derived directions - execution length, scenario novelty, skill
rarity - through a loop-engineered multi-agent harness with a rubric reviewer, an oracle
verifier, an invalid-test verifier and an adaptive-rubrics verifier, plus a human-in-the-loop
pass whose findings become new rubrics. An Evolution-Lineage scheduler admits the next
generation only when the current generation's aggregate pass rate exceeds "a predefined
threshold (τ = 6/8)".

Numbers, the authors' own:
- Terminal-Bench 2.1 peak accuracy against two baseline paradigms with the environment count
  held constant: Qwen3.6-27B "71.5% accuracy (at 170 RL steps), surpassing Co-evolution (62.9%
  at 130 steps) and Ensemble (60.0% at 90 steps)", "a 14.4 percentage point improvement over
  Co-evolution"; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B "64.9% accuracy (at 190 steps)" against "55.1%" and "52.8%",
  "an 18.0 percentage point improvement". The abstract states the same as "improving their
  performance by 14.4 and 18.0 percentage points on Terminal-Bench 2.1".
- Training cost grows with difficulty: turns "increased from approximately 50 to 190" and tokens
  "from roughly 50k to 232k" for the 27B model.
- Direction effects: *Length* "produced the largest decrease in pass rate (7.1 pp in 1-step
  effect) while generating the smallest total mutation rate (63.8%)"; *Scenario* and *Skill*
  produced larger turn increases (13.5 and 12.5 turns).
- Instruction mutation stays "high (95-100%)" at both high and max effort.

## Why it is in this line
It is the clearest 2026 statement of what "environment scaling" means as a programme, and it is
the source that makes the *quality over count* point from inside the scaling camp: holding the
number of training environments constant, changing how the environments are evolved moves
Terminal-Bench 2.1 by 14.4 and 18.0 points. It also prices the axis honestly - the tokens and
turns per trajectory rise with environment difficulty, which is the cost side this repository
would inherit.

## Quality and limits
Formal: arXiv preprint, Tencent Hunyuan team, full author list, no venue or peer review visible
in the record read. Every number is the authors' own, cited and not reproduced here. The
comparison is against their own re-implementations of the two baselines, and the report does not
give a seed count or variance for the peak accuracies.
