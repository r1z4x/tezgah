# Kim Sun-young 2012, translationese sentences in English-Korean translation (NIKL journal Saegugeosaenghwal 22(1))

**What it is and claims.** A national language institute defining translationese as the regular recurrence of traces that a text is translated, and documenting the English-to-Korean transfer families with a replacement for each: possessive `have` rendered as `gajida`, passive constructions with `-e uihae`, plural marker `-eul` used for every English `-s` (with the contrast between `chaegi manhta` and `chaekdeuri manhta`), prepositional phrases carried over as `-e gwanhaye`, `-robuteo`, and the too-to-not-to construction. It also reports that English translationese dominates Chinese and Japanese translationese in kind and frequency, and states that when a concept or culturally specific word has no Korean equivalent, the trace must be retained.

**How it changes the skill.** This is the model for a language rail's marker leg: a sourced family list with replacements, from a language's own institution rather than from an English detector paper. The last point is the exception rule that keeps the marker set from over-firing.

**Quality.** Institutional or community source read in session; the field's own authority, not a measurement.
