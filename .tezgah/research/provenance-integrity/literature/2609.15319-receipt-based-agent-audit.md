# Clean Scores, Buried Evidence, and Confident Wrong: A Receipt-Based Audit of Frontier Agentic QA

- why read: it is the closest published statement of the class this line was opened on -

## What it claims
- In a controlled data-room audit, burying the evidence reduced accuracy, increased
  forced declarations and cost per correct answer, and **confidence and benchmark
  calibration did not fully capture the wrong answers**.
- A documented production incident shows "fabricated structural claims can be mixed
  with accurate numeric tables" - the failure is not a wrong number but a number
  whose explanation was never measured.
- The recommended discipline is "claim-level receipts (statement-level provenance,
  not answer-level scores)", condition-aware scoring and human-adversarial
  verification - "an auditing discipline, not a leaderboard".
- The grader's own sensitivity is disclosed: several conclusions depend on a frozen
  containment grader, and the report states which survive blinded human review.

## What transfers to a hook-based harness
- **Statement-level provenance is the published shape.** The field this line added
  (`scope` on a results row and on a claim, not a score on the answer) is the same
  move the paper argues for: the receipt travels with the statement, because the
  answer-level score is what hid the defect.
- **A right number with an unmeasured explanation is the class.** This is why the
  control here is on the claim's wording and the row's input, not on arithmetic: the
  numbers in these incidents were accurate, so a numeric check alone cannot see it.
- **Disclose the grader's sensitivity.** The paper states which findings survive its
  grader's blind spots, which is the same discipline this line's report follows with
  its two numbers and its stated limits.

## What does not transfer
- Their setting is document QA with hidden evidence and a containment grader; a
  session here has no grader, so the receipt is the only mechanism, and its
  declaration is only as good as the producer's honesty (spec.md, class 1b).
