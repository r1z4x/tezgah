# UHP 2026-09-12 — Unified Harness Protocol

https://github.com/HarnessRouter/harnessrouter — `protocol/README.md` and
`protocol/versions/2026-09-12/`, version `2026-09-12`, status "Draft standard"; repository and
specification text read through the GitHub raw endpoints on 2026-09-20. Vendor: HarnessRouter
(https://harnessrouter.ai). Apache-2.0.

## What it is
An open standard for running complete agent harnesses as shared infrastructure. Its own
definition, which is the sentence this line has to take seriously: "A *harness* is a complete
agent runtime — a loop that plans, calls tools, edits files, and reports back. Codex, Claude
Code and Hermes are harnesses." UHP standardizes the harness-to-application interface: which
harness runs a task, its tools and skills, the session that survives between tasks, the files
returned, and cancellation. Its task surface is deliberately shaped like the OpenAI Responses
API - a conformant server MUST accept the documented subset of that request body and emit the
documented event vocabulary - and the unit of exchange is stated as a *job*, not a completion.

The chapters are architecture, lifecycle, harnesses, plugins, tasks, streaming, sessions, files,
errors, security and schema, with a machine-readable OpenAPI 3.1 + JSON Schema 2020-12 set and a
runnable conformance suite that defines "conformant". Two parts are **commercial** and are cited
as the vendor's, not as independent: the Community Edition product and the vendor's own
benchmark. The benchmark figure the vendor publishes is "99.8% lower cost, from 223 to 0.47
credits; end-to-end latency 3.2 times faster, from 4m 36s to 1m 25s" across **eight harness x
model configurations on the same task**, with the note that the lowest-cost and fastest
configurations vary by task. The methodology page is on the vendor's own site.

## Why it is in this line
UHP is the literature's opposite assignment of the word to this repository's own: here the
harness is the wrapper tezgah adds around a host, there the harness *is* the host-side runtime
and tezgah's kind of layer would be one of the "surrounding modules" the protocol unifies. That
contrast is the whole reason the boundary page exists, and UHP is also the only source here that
states the boundary as a wire contract - what a caller may assume about a harness - rather than
as prose.

## Quality and limits
Grey: a vendor's own specification and its own marketing surface, neither peer-reviewed nor
independent of the product it describes. The specification text is openly licensed and
versioned, so the *definition* and the *interface* can be read at first hand; the two numbers
above cannot - they come from the vendor's benchmark of its own product on one task, with the
methodology hosted by the vendor, and are recorded here as the vendor's claim and not as a
measurement. The status field says "Draft standard", so the interface may still change.
