## Claim, with its exact numbers

A population-level audit rather than a prompt-level one: 1,465,145 arXiv
preprints (Jan 2020 - Aug 2025, 44,107,529 references) plus three bibliometric
corpora including a 10% random sample of PubMed Central (374,807 manuscripts,
19,245,787 references), matched against a Semantic Scholar + OpenAlex index.

Pipeline rates as reported: the initial title match resolved **95.1%** of
references; a cleaning step reduced the unmatched set to **2.33%**, a title
extraction refinement to **1.54%**. The unmatched rate is **stable through late
2022** - the pre-LLM baseline of ordinary bibliographic error - and the excess
above that baseline after late 2022 is what the study attributes to hallucination.
Science is described as a "best-case domain" for detection, so the observed
prevalence is a conservative estimate for less structured domains.

## Why it matters here

The number that matters for a workspace is not the rate but the *verifiability*:
citation existence is "unambiguously verified against established databases",
which is why it is the one evidence class a checker can decide without reading
the claim. The layer already owns a checker and does not use it for this - E1's
P8.
