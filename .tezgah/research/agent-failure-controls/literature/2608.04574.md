## Detailed Research Report: When Memory Lies: An Empirical Study of Spatial Memory Staleness in VLM Agents

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The research paper "When Memory Lies: An Empirical Study of Spatial Memory Staleness in VLM Agents" was authored by Yushi Sun and Yanjie Zhang. Yushi Sun is affiliated with LIGHTSPEED, Shenzhen, China, and Yanjie Zhang is affiliated with The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, Hong Kong, China. Yanjie Zhang's contribution was completed during an internship at Tencent LIGHTSPEED.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents operating in dynamic environments. Recent advancements have led to the development of memory-augmented agents, such as Voyager, GITM, Reflexion, and MemGPT, which utilize persistent memory to accumulate skills, maintain hierarchical trajectories, store feedback, or manage structured knowledge. Existing evaluations of these systems primarily focus on aspects like memory construction, retrieval, and reuse. However, these studies largely overlook a critical failure mode: the silent invalidation, or "staleness," of stored spatial knowledge as the environment changes.

Concurrently, research has investigated the temporal validity of textual factual knowledge and methods for editing outdated associations within model weights. While these areas touch upon knowledge update mechanisms, they generally do not extend to spatial claims that become invalid and directly impact safety-critical actions in a physical or simulated environment.

The broader landscape also includes extensive work on spatial reasoning and hallucination benchmarks for VLMs. These benchmarks, including Spatial-Eval, SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, and HallusionBench, typically assess VLM behavior on static observations. They evaluate spatial perception, reasoning, planning, or low-level visual capabilities without considering scenarios where a stored spatial description of the past conflicts with a changed current observation.

This research positions itself at the intersection of these fields, specifically exploring the scenario where a previously acquired spatial claim becomes invalid and necessitates reconciliation with current observations before a safety-relevant action. It aims to bridge the gap between memory-augmented agents, knowledge validity, and spatial reasoning by studying how VLMs handle conflicting information from persistent, potentially stale spatial memory and real-time environmental observations. By focusing on this specific causal loop—entry-level auditing of stale spatial memory and its subsequent use in downstream navigation safety—the paper differentiates itself from prior work on reflection, embodied planning, and general change detection, which do not fully integrate these elements into a safety-critical context.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the behavior of memory-augmented VLM agents when their persistent spatial knowledge becomes outdated, or "stale," due to environmental changes. The authors are motivated by the observation that while memory is a central design pattern for capable agents, its stored spatial knowledge can silently become invalid. This raises a fundamental question: what occurs when an agent possesses a confident memory claim that directly contradicts a current observation, and can current models detect this conflict before it leads to a critical error?

A core motivation stems from the potential safety implications of stale memory. The paper highlights a scenario in a FrozenLake environment where an agent's memory indicates a cell is safe (frozen ice), but the environment has changed, and the cell is now a lethal hole. Acting on this stale memory carries a tangible cost, exemplified in the study by a GPT-4o agent that trusts raw stale memory, experiencing more than twice the death rate compared to an agent given no memory at all (74.4% vs. 28.0% death). This illustrates a direct "safety liability" associated with un-audited stale memory.

To address these concerns, the research sets out to answer five linked questions:

1.  **Staleness Identification**: Can current VLMs accurately identify stale spatial claims?
2.  **Modality Transfer**: Does this ability to detect staleness transfer effectively from text-based observations to visual observations?
3.  **Safety Cost**: What are the safety consequences of consuming raw, unfiltered stale memory?
4.  **Audit Efficacy**: Can an explicit, read-time memory audit effectively remove the safety costs associated with stale memory?
5.  **Residual Failure Attribution**: Once an audit mechanism is reliable, are any remaining failures due to limitations in stale-label accuracy, or are they primarily attributable to deficiencies in action selection based on the filtered memory?

By empirically investigating these questions, the study aims to characterize the mechanisms of memory staleness as a safety failure mode and to identify the challenges associated with reliable visual grounding and appropriate action selection in the presence of memory-observation conflicts.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The study employs **SpatialSTALE**, a dynamic 8×8 FrozenLake gridworld testbed, designed to control and observe memory, world state, and task outcomes. This environment allows for the isolation of the mechanism of spatial memory staleness and its impact on agent behavior.

**Experimental Procedure:**
Each instance in SpatialSTALE proceeds through four stages:
1.  **Grid and Memory Snapshot Generation**: An initial 8×8 grid ($g_0$) with approximately 25% holes, fixed start (S) and goal (G) cells, and at least one valid path is generated. A memory snapshot is created by simulating 20 random walks on $g_0$; each visited position records its type, and unvisited cells receive synthetic entries, ensuring exactly 64 memory entries (one per cell). Each entry is a natural language claim (e.g., "SAFE at (2,5): Frozen ice, safe to walk").
2.  **World Change**: Non-terminal cells (Frozen or Hole) are programmatically flipped either before or during navigation, while preserving a route to the goal.
3.  **Memory Auditing**: Given the original textual memory and a current grid observation (presented as either a coordinate-labeled text list or a 384×384 rendered image), the model is tasked with returning a binary "valid" or "stale" judgment for each memory entry. An entry is deemed stale if its stored type differs from the current cell type at that position. Performance is measured using Precision, Recall, and F1 scores.
4.  **Navigation**: The agent then navigates the changed grid using either raw, filtered, or no memory. Outcomes recorded include success, death (stepping on a hole), path length, and reward.

**Controlled Change Regimes (Difficulty Levels):**
The study uses three regimes to vary when and how changes occur:
*   **L1 (Random spot)**: 5 to 7 scattered cell changes occur before navigation. Detection is run once.
*   **L2 (Local clusters)**: 12 to 16 clustered cell changes occur before navigation. Detection is run once.
*   **L3 (Online dynamic)**: Changes occur incrementally during navigation (2 cells flipped every fifth step with 70% probability). Detection is re-run after each realized event.

**Input Modalities:**
*   **Text Mode**: Current full grid observation is a coordinate-labeled list of cell types.
*   **Vision Mode**: Current full grid observation is a color-coded 384×384 RGB image with coordinate labels. Memory entries remain textual in both modes.

**Memory Strategies for Navigation:**
Four strategies, sharing the same action space and step budget, are compared:
1.  **NoMemory**: Memory portion of the prompt is empty. Acts as a memoryless control baseline.
2.  **NoFilter**: All 64 original memory entries are passed to the navigator, unfiltered. Measures the impact of trusting raw stale memory.
3.  **SelfVerify**: The model performs a single, full-store self-check before navigation to remove inconsistent entries; no further audit occurs during the episode.
4.  **OMCD (Observation-Memory Consistency Detector)**: This is the transparent, controlled filtering intervention. It audits memory entries in batches (B=10) and, for L3, re-audits after realized environmental changes. Predicted-stale entries are removed from the active memory view.
5.  **Oracle labels (Ablation)**: This condition uses ground-truth stale labels through the OMCD filtering interface, allowing assessment of the impact of label quality.

**Models and Scale:**
The study evaluates six VLM agents:
*   Closed-source: GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4.6, Qwen3.6-Plus.
*   Open-weight: GLM-5.1, InternVL3-2B, InternVL3-8B.
A total of 1,800 detection runs (6 models × 50 seeds × 3 regimes × 2 modalities) were conducted. For text-mode navigation, 12,000 episodes were run (4 LLM navigators × 50 shared seeds × 3 regimes × 4 strategies × 5 episodes per model). A smaller exploratory preview of vision-navigation results (10 seeds × 3 episodes) is also presented. Statistical significance is assessed using paired Wilcoxon signed rank tests at $\alpha = 0.001$.

**Technical Details:**
The closed-source models were accessed via their official APIs. Open-weight VLMs were run locally using vLLM for batched inference. The environment and evaluation harness were implemented in Python 3.11.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study yielded three core findings regarding spatial-memory staleness in VLM agents:

**5.1. Text Solvability Does Not Necessarily Transfer to Visual Grounding.**
*   **Text Staleness Detection**: In text mode, where both observation and memory are linguistic, capable models demonstrated high accuracy. GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4.6, Qwen3.6-Plus, and GLM-5.1 achieved average F1 scores above 0.88 for L1 and L2 regimes, although performance degraded on L3 with incremental changes. The smaller InternVL3 checkpoints (2B and 8B) exhibited lower text F1 scores (approx. 0.22 and 0.31, respectively).
*   **Visual Transfer Variability**: When presented with identical grids in vision mode (384x384 rendered images), the ability to detect staleness varied significantly across models. Qwen3.6-Plus maintained near text-level accuracy (0.887 F1), showing only a minor, non-significant F1 drop. Claude-Sonnet-4.6 experienced moderate degradation (∆F1 = -0.134). GPT-4o showed severe degradation (∆F1 = -0.598, p < 1e-9), while GLM-5.1’s performance collapsed (∆F1 = -0.830, p < 1e-9), yielding an F1 of 0.067. This disparity indicates a processing failure rather than unreadable input, localizing the issue to the visual auditing stage.
*   **GLM-5.1 Visual Failure Profile**: GLM-5.1’s visual audit was characterized by confident, fluent assertions that often contradicted the image content (e.g., stating a cell was "light blue frozen ice" when rendered as a "dark hole"). It flagged memory-safe and deadly-stale entries at nearly identical rates (5.0% vs. 4.5%), suggesting its decisions were memory-dominated and largely insensitive to visual contradiction.
*   **Consequence for Vision Navigation**: When the visual audit proved unreliable, filtering memory provided no consistent downstream benefit to navigation success. Models with poor visual detection (GPT-4o, Claude, GLM-5.1) achieved at most 0.40 success, with OMCD offering no consistent improvement, unlike Qwen3.6-Plus, which maintained high vision detection and success rates.

**5.2. Unfiltered Stale Memory is a Safety Liability, but an Explicit Audit Removes Most of the Tax.**
*   **Safety Cost of Stale Memory**: Consuming raw, unfiltered stale memory was found to be a substantial safety liability. In the primary L2 GPT-4o text navigation setting, trusting raw stale memory (NoFilter) resulted in a 74.4% death rate, significantly higher than the 28.0% death rate observed when no memory was provided (NoMemory). This indicated that stale memory made the agent 2.7 times deadlier.
*   **Effectiveness of Auditing**: The OMCD intervention, which explicitly audits and filters stale memory entries, substantially mitigated this safety cost. Across all four text-mode navigation models (GPT-4o, Claude, Qwen, GLM-5.1), OMCD consistently reduced death rates compared to NoFilter (e.g., GPT-4o L1 death rate dropped from 46.8% to 21.6%).
*   **Remediation and Label Quality**: An ablation using Oracle labels (ground-truth stale labels applied via OMCD's pipeline) demonstrated that the stale-memory tax is remediable, with Oracle raising L2/L3 success by 18-24 percentage points over NoFilter (p < 10^-5). However, no detectable difference in navigation success was observed between OMCD's learned text labels and Oracle labels (|∆| ≤ 0.02, p > 0.2), suggesting that in the 8x8 text setting, the learned audit was close to label-saturating for the downstream task.

**5.3. After Filtering is Reliable, the Remaining Bottleneck is Acting on the Filtered Memory.**
*   **Trajectory Analysis**: Outcome-conditioned trajectory lengths provided insight into failure mechanisms. NoFilter deaths for GPT-4o and Claude typically occurred in sharply shorter trajectories (8.5 and 7.8 steps, respectively) compared to successful runs (14.5 and 14.6 steps). This "rush-into-hole" signature suggested that stale memory directly steered agents into lethal cells early. In contrast, Claude's NoMemory deaths were the longest (16.1 steps), indicating prolonged exploration before eventual failure. OMCD deaths generally fell between these two extremes.
*   **Decoupling Detection F1 and Navigation Success**: In the primary GPT-4o text study, there was no significant correlation between per-seed detection F1 and OMCD navigation success (Pearson r ranged from +0.005 to +0.060, all p > 0.67). Furthermore, all OMCD L2 deaths on stale cells occurred in runs where the stale entry had been correctly detected and removed. These findings suggest that once the memory audit is reliable and stale entries are filtered, the primary bottleneck shifts from detection accuracy to the agent's action selection policy given the filtered memory view. Improving stale labels alone did not significantly address the remaining action-selection errors in this observed setting.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of VLM agent capabilities and limitations, particularly in safety-critical applications involving dynamic environments.

**Identification of a Safety Failure Mode:** The study explicitly identifies and characterizes spatial-memory staleness as a significant safety failure mode for memory-augmented VLM agents. It demonstrates that agents relying on unfiltered, outdated spatial knowledge can perform worse than agents operating without any memory, leading to quantifiable safety risks (e.g., increased death rates in navigation tasks).

**Empirical Characterization and Quantification:** The work provides a detailed empirical characterization of modality-dependent auditing and the "stale-memory safety tax." By conducting a comprehensive study across multiple state-of-the-art models, various change regimes, and both text and vision modalities, it offers robust evidence of the problem's prevalence and its differential impact on diverse VLM architectures. The use of outcome-conditioned trajectories further refines the understanding of how different memory strategies lead to specific failure types (e.g., direct "rush-into-hole" versus "explore-until-death").

**Practical Intervention and Bottleneck Localization:** The introduction and evaluation of OMCD demonstrate that an explicit, read-time filtering intervention can effectively mitigate much of the safety cost associated with stale memory in text-based interactions. Importantly, the study isolates key bottlenecks: reliable visual grounding for auditing, and subsequent action selection over filtered memory. The finding that Oracle labels offer no significant additional benefit over learned text filtering in the tested 8x8 setting redirects research attention towards improving navigation policies that act on (potentially incomplete but accurate) filtered information, rather than solely perfecting staleness detection.

**Implications for VLM Development:** The severe degradation of visual staleness detection in several capable VLMs highlights a critical gap in their visual grounding capabilities when faced with reconciling conflicting past knowledge and current visual observations. This suggests a need for enhanced VLM architectures or training paradigms that better integrate visual input with linguistic memory for consistency checking.

**Future Research Directions:** The study provides a foundation for future work by outlining several extensions to the SpatialSTALE benchmark, including larger grids, the introduction of distractor memory entries, multi-step memory edits, multi-agent staleness scenarios, continuous-control environments, and vision-only memory. These extensions aim to increase task difficulty and broaden the ecological validity of findings.

**Reproducibility:** The public release of code, map sets, model traces, and reproducibility pipelines supports continued research and validation of these findings by the broader scientific community. This transparency facilitates the development of more robust and safe memory-augmented AI agents.
