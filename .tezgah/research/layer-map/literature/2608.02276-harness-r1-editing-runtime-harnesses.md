Hao Wang, Wenxiang Jiao, Yuan Lu, Yi Guo, Weiwen Liu, Weinan Zhang — 2026-08-03, cs.AI.
Abstract fetched through the arXiv API; the same record is in OpenAlex as

## What it is
Harness-R1 makes editing the runtime harness a *learned* capability instead of a fixed editor's
job. A separate 9B "harness engineer" is post-trained with online reinforcement learning: it
turns batches of the target agent's failures into validated executable patches, and fresh
same-batch reruns of the frozen target supply the outcome reward, so only the engineer moves.
Cold-start supervised fine-tuning initializes the editing policy, then group-relative policy
optimization trains it online.

Reported: across WebShop, ALFWorld and DBBench, vanilla Qwen3.5-9B rises from 44.3% to 53.6%
(+9.3pp). After directly fine-tuning the target agent, a target-specific engineer raises the
average further from 59.2% to 64.2% (+5.0pp). The claimed direction is co-evolving the engineer
with the target.

## Why it is in this line
Where AHE evolves a harness through a hand-designed loop, Harness-R1 makes the *editing policy*
itself a trained artifact that consumes failures. For the boundary question this is the
sharpest case of a harness as the editable, executable layer that sits between model and task:
it is edited as code, as a patch, and its edits are judged by the target's task success rather
than by anything inside the model. It also supplies a second, independent sense in which a
harness can be "complete": executable runtime, not just a wrapper object.

## Quality and limits
Formal: arXiv preprint with a stated author list and category; no venue or peer review visible
in the record read. All figures are the authors' own, cited and not reproduced. The setting is
