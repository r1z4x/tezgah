# Meldrum 2009, translationese-specific linguistic characteristics - a corpus study of contemporary Japanese translationese

**What it is and claims.** A comparable corpus of 16 translated and 18 originally-Japanese bestsellers (377,591 vs 282,369 characters) quantifies the tells: `kanojo` (she) 19.4 vs 0.3, `kare` (he) 8.1 vs 5.5, `karera` (they) 1.9 vs 0.4 per 10,000 characters, and katakana loanwords 224.7 vs 82.4, about 2.7 times as many in translated text. It also tests and does not confirm some claimed features, and cites the in-Japanese practitioner literature with page numbers.

**How it changes the skill.** Two things: the pronoun numbers are the same mechanism as the Turkish redundant-pronoun rule, measured; and the katakana figure is a measured keep-or-translate effect - translated Japanese reaches for the loanword where original Japanese does not - which is the reverse of the plaza-dili case and shows the decision is per language, not per principle.

**Quality.** Formal: a standard, a peer-reviewed paper or an academic-press record.
