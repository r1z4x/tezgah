# Academie francaise, Terminologie and Questions de langue (the Loi Toubon apparatus)

**What it is and claims.** A normative doctrine for the axis, plus numbers: terminology is not lexicography and an equivalent is not a translation, with a literal translation warned against as equivocal - `class action` becomes `action de groupe`, not `action de classe`. Recommended refusals include `numerique` for digital, `memoire vive` for RAM, `courriel` for e-mail, `jeune pousse` for start-up. Measured: anglicisms are about 2.5 percent of the common vocabulary, and 686 of 38,897 headwords in the ninth edition (1.76 percent) are of English origin, behind Italian at 753 (1.93). It records both directions honestly - `ordinateur` and `logiciel` won, and `confortable` is called a very necessary anglicism with no equivalent, while `feedback`, `speech` and `customiser` are listed as avoidable.

**How it changes the skill.** The per-language policy in citable form, and the proof that the decision is two-way: some loans are the standard term, some are avoidable, and the authority distinguishes them item by item with a reason.

**Quality.** Institutional or survey source read in session; prescriptive or descriptive, not peer-reviewed measurement.
