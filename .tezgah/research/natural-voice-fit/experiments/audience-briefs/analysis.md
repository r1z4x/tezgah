# H2 - a register brief against one generic audience line

**What ran.** `harness/run.py --track h2`, transport `host`, six Turkish fixture drafts, two
per register. Arm A was told only that the audience is general professional readers; arm B
received `harness/briefs.json`'s brief for that register - reader, what they want first, the
vocabulary that belongs to them, a length bound - and was told the draft is checked against
it. Everything else, including the shipped contract, was identical.

**What the rows say.** Mean audience-fit delta **+1.96** on a 0-5 rubric, 95% CI
**[+1.38, +2.58]**, well clear of zero and of the +0.5 the protocol predicted. Every one of
the six items moved in the same direction, the smallest by +1.0.

**Which claim it moves.** C01, supported: decomposing the reader into features moves the
judged fit by about two points on a five-point scale.

**What it does not show.** The judges were given the same brief they were scoring against,
so part of the delta is the brief being legible to the judge rather than the draft serving
the reader better - the honest reading is "the brief makes the intent checkable", which is
what the controlled-generation literature predicts, but a test with a reader who has not seen
the brief is missing. Six fixture drafts, one generator, no human reader. The length bound in
each brief is my own composition, not a measured natural length for the register.
