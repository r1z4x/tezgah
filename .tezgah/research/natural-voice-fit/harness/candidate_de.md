## German drafts

Applies when the draft is German (`de`). The rail is the German orthographic
authority and the German plain-language supplement to ISO 24495-1
(DIN 8581-1), not a translated copy of the English word list.

**Prefer the established German term where one exists.** A loanword used where
German has its own term is the defect this section names, and it is measured,
not assumed: a German survey found English words and slogans fully understood
by fewer than half of the respondents, and business writing that reaches for
`CEO` where `Geschäftsführer` is meant, or `staff` where `Belegschaft` is meant,
is the documented pattern.

- `Release Notes` -> `Versionshinweise`; `Root-Cause-Analyse` ->
  `Ursachenanalyse`; `Churn-Rate` -> `Abwanderungsquote`; `Pricing-Modell` ->
  `Preismodell`; `Task` -> `Aufgabe`; `Owner` -> `Verantwortlicher`;
  `Alert-Schwellwert` -> `Warnschwelle`; `Incident Summary` ->
  `Störungsbericht`; `Queue-Management` -> `Warteschlangenverwaltung`;
  `allokiert` -> `zugewiesen`; `supported` -> `unterstützt`; `Adoption` ->
  `Einführung`; `Ask` -> `Anliegen`.

**Keep what must stay verbatim, and never Germanise it.** Code identifiers, file
and script names, URIs, version strings, unit symbols, acronyms in use, product
and company names: reproduced exactly, not respelled, not translated and not
transliterated. Product names are never translated by meaning. Unit symbols are
mathematical objects - no plural, no full stop.

**Do not stack noun compounds into a string.** Four or more nouns in a row is
not German thoroughness; unpack it into a verb phrase.

**Cut the bureaucratic setup.** `In diesem Zusammenhang ist festzuhalten,
dass`, `es muss betont werden, dass`, `darüber hinaus ist anzumerken, dass`:
state the fact instead, then the consequence.

**Do not translate proper nouns or standards.** Organisation names are written
as their organisations write them; standard and version designations are not
re-worded.
