# Findings

## What we know

- **The pack's descriptions are trigger-stripped where its invocation model says
  they should be, and tezgah's router selects on exactly that.** 22 of the 38
  upstream `SKILL.md` return a line with no `Use when …` sentence (C01), all 22
  user-invoked; the shipped control returns 14 of 14. Nine of the 22 return 100+
  characters, so it is the sentence, not the length.
- **Adopting the pack is cheap in always-on bytes — the opposite of the
  assumption this line started from.** 6180 B for 38 descriptions against 9000 B
  for tezgah's 14 (C02), because tezgah averages 624.1 characters per
  description and the pack 142.0.
- **The craft reference is affordable.** `writing-for-agents` plus
  `SKILL-MECHANICS.md` is 13515 B, 0.38× CONTRACT (C06b); most of it is reference
  read only behind a pointer.
- **Two of the three conflicts are packaging problems, not body problems.** The
  float the suite rejects appears in 0 of 38 bodies and 1 of 66 markdown files
  (C03c, which supersedes C03 and cites the artifact the counts live in); a body
  carrying it fails exactly one test when grafted, and the same
  graft without it fails none (C05).
- **One conflict is a hole, not a disagreement.** The pack's `triage` requires an
  AI-attribution line on every posted comment; tezgah's contract bans AI
  attribution on any host; and `attribution()` returns false for it, while
  denying the `Co-Authored-By` control in the same run (C04). Nothing that runs
  would stop an adopted `triage` from publishing attributed comments.
- **The failure mode the pack would import is already installed.** 19 of the 114
  `SKILL.md` under `skills/` return a non-trigger line (C07) — inside the
  vendored `ai-research` bucket, which the router collapses to a count line.

- **The one pack mechanism that is measurably absent is the provoke-the-failure
  rule.** The judge puts P(yes) at 0.04 while its two controls land at 0.99 and
  0.98, and the marker probe agrees at 0 hits across 49344 bytes (C08), so a
  treatment clause has something to add rather than restating the contract.
- **Two of the three candidate mechanisms are already carried.** 17 prohibition
  markers against 9 positive-target ones (C10) means the phrasing lever exists but
  is a minority instrument, and 4 of the 14 descriptions already say what the
  skill is *not* for (C09).

- **The clause is unmeasured after two rounds, for two different infrastructure
  reasons.** Round 1: the arm was not in the tree orx copies (C11). Round 2: the
  provider refused every call with `errorStatus` 402, so all 300 rows are
  zero-usage failures at $0.00 (C13). Neither round says anything about the clause.
- **The H4 block produced no measurement of the clause, and one corrected number.**
  The treatment arm's 100 jobs were all refused (a deployment failure, not a
  result), while the armed control's false-completion share is 2 of its 95 failed
  runs = 0.021 against the bare anchor's 19 of 75 = 0.253 (half its labelled failed
  runs against the anchor's: **2 of 46 = 0.043** against 19 of 69 = 0.275 excluding
  timeouts, the 2 resting on the 5 armed failures that wrote any final message of
  the 41 that wrote none) — a direction the integrity rules predict on a thin
  denominator (C11, C12c). The row as first folded said 3, 0.032 and 0.065; those
  do not reproduce from the run's own rows under the pinned or the current
  `claims()` predicate, and C12c says so (the numbers were re-folded on 2026-09-22).

- **The clause the measurements pointed at was run, and it changed nothing
  measurable.** Round 3's treatment passed 94 of 100 against the armed control's 89
  (intervals overlapping), both armed arms fired the Stop rule 74 times, and the
  false-completion shares tied at 1.000 on denominators of one labelled run each
  (C14, C15).

## Patterns

- A skill's description is written for the harness that reads it [C01] [C07], and
  a migration between harnesses silently changes what it does: the pack's
  user-invoked one-liners are correct in Claude Code and routeless in a router
  that selects a trigger sentence.
- The guards that fire are the ones with a corpus [C03c] [C05]: `tests/test_skills.py`
  reads `skills/*/SKILL.md`, so it caught nothing until the text was *in* a body —
  while the attribution rule, whose subject is the phrasing, has no anchor for
  "generated by AI" [C04].
- A cost argument must name its unit [C02] [C06b] [C01]. Bytes always-on, bytes on
  demand, and router-line extractability are three different quantities, and only
  the last one moves against this pack.

## Lessons

- Measure the corpus before predicting about it: this line's first prediction
  (upstream ≥ 2× tezgah's bytes) was refuted by a factor of three, and the first
  control run walked 114 files where the protocol said 14.
- A protocol's control has to be named by the same rule the instrument applies.
  "This repository's 14 shipped skills" is a list; `skills/` is a tree; the
  difference is a vendor directory.
- An assumed conflict is worth one executable probe. Two of the three predicted
  collisions were in the packaging text (C03c) and one turned out to be a policy
  hole nothing enforces (C04).

## Open questions

- Which mechanism earns an arm-bench effect block (H4)? Answered as far as a
  measurement can: the provoke-the-failure clause, pre-registered with its clause,
  arm, metric and falsifier in `experiments/E4-mechanism-map/h4-block.md`. Not
  run: the harness is unhealthy at the checked-out commit, the lab tree is on
  `benchmarks/lab` rather than the baseline, and the orx node branches are gone.
- Does `attribution()` want an anchor for "generated by AI" / "generated by
  <model>"? C04 says the current anchor set ("generated with") is narrower than
  the ban the contract states; whether to widen it is a user decision, and
  widening it would refuse the pack's mandated line.
- Would a corpus that can price a one-sentence clause look different from these two
tasks? Round 3's label rate (1 labelled failure in 11) is the binding limit on the
false-completion column, and no round so far has fixed it.
- Does the clause deserve a second round? The armed contract already sits at
  0.021-0.043 false completion on this instrument, so the headroom the clause was
  aimed at is small, and the round's timeout ceiling (49 of 100 armed runs) makes
  the pass-rate family unusable until the block runs with less parallelism.
- Would a trigger-sentence rewrite of the 22 fallback descriptions (E1's list) be
  enough to make an adopted pack routable, or does the router want a per-skill
  exemption for user-invoked skills?
