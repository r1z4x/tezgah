# Anglicisms in German business communication (Bruckner 2020, Linguaculture) and the Denglisch record

**What it is and claims.** The academic treatment of the same phenomenon in German: Denglisch is the pejorative term and Anglizismus the neutral one; German firms use CEO instead of Geschaeftsfuehrer and staff instead of Belegschaft; a 2003 survey found English words and slogans were fully understood by fewer than half of German respondents. The documented failure cases cut both ways - a shoulder bag advertised as `body bag` (a corpse bag in English), and deliberate Germanisations that failed (`Komputer`, `Zwischennetz`) while `Computer` is standard next to `Rechner`.

**How it changes the skill.** Two rules fall out for the audience axis: an English term can fail to communicate to a German reader even when it looks professional, and a forced native coinage can fail even when a body recommends it. Both are measured rather than assumed.

**Quality.** Formal: a standard, a peer-reviewed paper or an institutional framework.
