## What it is
Environment Harness (EnvHarness) is a programmable layer of plug-in components that wraps a
*static* environment and reshapes its behaviour without modifying the underlying logic. Its
components work at the environment's own `reset()` and `step()` interfaces: `Stage` rewrites
the initial state `s_0`, `Contract` rewrites the action space, observation space or
transition function, `Chain` composes an environment with a second one into a longer episode.
The stated invariant is that every reshaped environment **retains its original verifier** -
"ensuring every reshaped environment retains its original verifier" (abstract).

EnvRigger is the automation, and its loop is the part this line needs. It treats the target
policy as a black box and runs four stages: *Observe* (collect execution trajectories of the
policy in the current environment), *Diagnose* (read root causes - repetitive loops, parse
failures, misread tool constraints - and decide whether the environment should be eased or
made harder), *Write* (synthesize candidate `Stage`/`Contract` components targeting the
diagnosis), *Validate* (**"Fresh rollouts of the policy are run in E'"**; the candidate is
accepted only if it "cultivates missing capabilities and remains solvable", rejected if it is
unsolvable or not challenging, and otherwise refined back through Write).

Numbers, all the authors' own and none reproduced here:
- "up to a 9.0-point improvement on held-out instances with 9.8% fewer execution steps" across
  "five benchmarks in four domains" (abstract); the report attributes the 9.0 points to
  ALFWorld held-out tasks.
- On SWE-bench Verified, the report records "+2.70 success rate with -5.40 agent steps" in the
  note already held by `.tezgah/research/layer-map`, and a step reduction "from 53.6 to 49.6, a
  9.8% reduction".
- Against other environment generators: "outperforms GenEnv (on ALFWorld, by 5.7 points
  average, 8.5 points OOD) and SWE-smith (on SWE-bench Verified, by 2.46 points success rate
  with 5.11 fewer steps)", and the summary sentence is explicit that this contradicts scaling:
  "targeting diagnosed vulnerabilities is more effective than merely scaling training episodes
  through specialized generation".
- The scaling curve, which is the closest thing to a published environment-count/capability
  relation in this set: as the number of available training environments rises, the
  EnvHarness-customized resolved rate on SWE-bench Verified "improv[es] from 47.67% to 54.79%
  (a 7.12-point gain)", while "both original and generated environments flattened out at higher
  environment counts".
- RL: ALFWorld in-distribution success 87.9% against 81.4% for original environments; WebShop
  "79.2 vs. 75.6" score and "67.4% vs. 66.0%" success.
- Cross-model: "2.7 to 3.7 absolute points" over original environments for Gemini 3.1
  Flash-Lite, Qwen3.6 27B, Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Sonnet 4.6.

## Why it is in this line
It is the environment-side layer the question asks about, and the one source in this set whose
loop is a *designer loop validated by fresh rollouts* rather than a training loop: components
are accepted on a rollout in the reshaped environment, not on a weight update. Its relatives,
as named by this paper, are the generation pipelines it beats - GenEnv and SWE-smith - which is
the contrast between generating environments and reshaping them. It also supplies the
discipline this repository would have to copy if it ever wrapped the environment side: the
verifier is left alone.

## Quality and limits
Formal: arXiv preprint with a full author list and affiliations (Google Cloud AI Research,
Washington University in St. Louis, UNC Chapel Hill), a named code repository, no venue or peer
review visible in the record read. The verifier-preserving claim is a design statement by the
authors, not something verified here. The scaling curve is reported without a seed count or
variance beside it, and the benchmark numbers are cited, never measured on this repository.
