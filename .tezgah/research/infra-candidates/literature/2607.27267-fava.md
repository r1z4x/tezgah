# FAVA: Formal Authorization for Verified Agents with Evidence-Backed Permission Graphs

- id: alphaXiv 2607.27267 (arXiv preprint), 2026-07-29
- authors: Yifan Zhang, Xinkui Zhao, Sai Liu, Hengxuan Lou, Guanjie Cheng, Chang Liu (Zhejiang University)
- url: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.27267
- read: alphaXiv report, 2026-09-19

## Claim

Agent authorization cannot be static: trajectories are probabilistic and the
security requirement is context-dependent. FAVA splits the job - an LLM extracts
a structured **Permission IR** (intent, assets, actions, obligations, sinks, each
linked to an evidence span), a deterministic lowering pass turns it into an
evidence-backed graph, and an **SMT solver** authorizes before every effectful
action, returning a counterexample when it refuses. The graph is monotonically
updated with observed runtime facts, forcing **re-authorization**, which they
frame as the remedy for LLM *context amnesia*.

## The numbers that matter to us

Empirical study of open-source repositories carrying `CLAUDE.md` / `AGENT.md`:

- **90% enforce sequence-dependent temporal constraints** (e.g. "do not commit
  before running tests").
- **over 40% incorporate nested conditional logic** (e.g. "allow internet access
  only when querying the official API").

They also survey the field's failure modes: prompt-only policies cannot enforce;
keyword filters and static allowlists ignore data flow and context; OS sandboxes
lack the semantic view.

## Why it matters here

**This is the largest measured gap between tezgah and real instruction files.**
tezgah's rules are all per-call predicates. The 90% figure says the dominant
constraint shape in the wild is temporal ("tests before commit"), and tezgah has
no rule of that shape - its closest is the active-task phase, which is a
per-call allowlist that happens to be read-only for a while.

The design lesson is also concrete and cheap compared with FAVA's SMT authorizer:

- the **obligation** field ("a precondition that must be met") is exactly the
  missing rule kind, and it needs no solver - a ledger row and one predicate;
- **re-authorization on every effectful action against the accumulating trace**
  is what tezgah's gate already does (`consent_mark`, `unspent_grant` read the
  ledger tail); the missing part is obligations, not the mechanism.
