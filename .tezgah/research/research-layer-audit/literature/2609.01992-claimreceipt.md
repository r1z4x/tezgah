## Claim

Two named properties decide whether a reported number can be trusted, and neither
follows from byte integrity (logs, hashes, signatures):

- **Sufficiency** - can the claim be *recomputed exactly* from what was retained?
- **Coverage** - is what was retained the *complete set* of committed experiments,
  or a favourable subset? This is answered by a manifest committed **before**
  outcomes are observed ("a cryptographic preregistration"), which makes later
  omissions visible.

Numbers that make the point: **generic logs, transcripts and signed receipts
determined 0 of 12 claims**; an opened per-transaction receipt did 6 of 12; the
full claim-relative schema did 12 of 12 within its declared scope. A field-group
ablation showed each of 13 groups was necessary for at least one claim. The
verifier caught 11 of 11 frozen semantic faults - cross-arm schema drift,
scorer/version drift, selective reporting, pseudo-replication, missing terminal
evidence - with 0 false positives on 8 benign variations, and returns
Pass/Invalid/Inconclusive rather than pass/fail.

## Why it matters here

This is the sharpest available statement of what the tezgah workspace is missing.
Its `claims.jsonl` is a signed-receipt-shaped record with no recompute path: the
`proof` is a path that exists (E1 P4/P5/P6), never a quantity that can be
recomputed, and no manifest fixes the set of experiments a line committed to - so
a line can run ten experiments and record the one that worked (E1 P1/P3, and the
coverage gap has no counterpart check at all).
