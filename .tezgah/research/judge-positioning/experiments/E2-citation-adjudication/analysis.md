# E2 - what the citation adjudication test produced

**The rows.** Eleven citations, one model call each, all over the real tree
(scope `real`): seven hand-labelled `shows`, two `does-not-show`, two `unclear`.
Agreement on the nine clear items: **5 of 9 = 0.56**, against the pre-registered
floor of 0.9. The model flagged seven citations as drifted; **two** of those are
drifted by the hand labels and **four** are not, which puts precision on the
flagged set at **0.29** - so both halves of the criterion failed.

**Which claim it moves.** C5-citation-surface was untested and is now **refuted**
as written: one batched call per citation does not decide this remainder at a
precision worth acting on. A superseding row records it, naming this experiment
as the evidence.

**What the run does not show.** The verdict distribution is skewed - the model
answered `does-not-show` seven times out of eleven - and a single-call format that
offers two phrases with no worked example invites that bias, so the honest reading
is "not decidable *in this format*" rather than "not decidable at all". The two
`unclear` items are fragments whose citing sentence does not say what the citation
promises, and they are excluded from the agreement figure rather than counted
either way. The hand labels are one reader's judgement, made in this session, and
eleven citations out of 745 is a sample. A stronger version of the same test -
both verdict orders, one worked example, a larger sample - was not run and is the
cheapest next step if anyone wants to rescue the surface.
